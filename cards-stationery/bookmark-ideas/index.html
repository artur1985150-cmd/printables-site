What size is a bookmark?

There is no single official size, but print services agree on the usual ones. 4OVER4 names 2 x 6 inches as the most common, with 2 x 7 and 2 x 8 inches as the other standard sizes; Overnight Prints also calls 2 x 6 one of the most common.

Size (inches) Size (mm, approx.) Good for
2 x 6 51 x 152 Everyday default, fits most books
2 x 7 51 x 178 A bit more room for a quote
2 x 8 51 x 203 A tassel hole or full-height artwork
1.5 x 7 38 x 178 A slim, minimal look
2.5 x 8.5 64 x 216 A bold statement piece

How to set up a printable bookmark

A bookmark is a tall, narrow page, so small mistakes show. The setup 4OVER4 gives:

  1. Choose the trim size, for example 2 x 6 in.
  2. Add 0.125 in of bleed on every side if colour or a pattern runs to the edge. A 2 x 6 bookmark becomes a 2.25 x 6.25 in file.
  3. Keep text and anything important inside a safe margin, about 0.125 to 0.25 in from the trim line.
  4. Use images sharp enough for print. Overnight Prints gives 300 PPI as a common target for printed raster images.

At home, print at actual size rather than "fit to page", so the strip comes out the size you designed. Our guide on printing at home explains that setting. Then cut along the trim line with a ruler and craft knife or a paper trimmer.

DIY bookmark ideas from things at home

Most of these need card, scissors and glue:

  • Origami corner bookmark: a folded paper triangle that slips over the corner of a page. Add ears and a face to make an animal.
  • Ribbon or tassel strip: a card strip with a punched hole at the top and a loop of ribbon, embroidery thread or yarn.
  • Collage strip: layers of old greeting cards, wrapping paper, maps or magazine pictures glued onto card.
  • Pressed leaf or flower strip: a flat, fully dried leaf placed on card and covered with clear packing tape or a laminating pouch.
  • Felt bookmark: two felt layers glued or stitched together, cut into a shape.
  • Envelope corner: cut the corner off a used envelope and decorate it; it slips over the page corner.
  • Craft stick bookmark: a painted wooden stick with a paper shape glued to the top.
  • Washi tape strip: stripes of patterned tape across a plain card strip.
  • Stamp or doodle strip: a plain 2 x 6 strip filled with repeated stamps or drawings.

If you keep a junk journal, offcuts from its pages make a matching bookmark, and sticker sheets you make yourself work well for decorating plain strips.

Bookmark design ideas

On a 2 x 6 strip, less goes further than on a card. Design ideas that suit the narrow shape:

  • A short quote set vertically, with one small drawing
  • A single tall illustration: a tree, a lighthouse, a giraffe, a stack of books
  • A reading tracker: numbered boxes or lines to tick off chapters or books
  • A pattern on one side and a short note or the reader's name on the other
  • A seasonal design: leaves, snowflakes, flowers
  • A dark background with light lettering, which stands out when the bookmark sticks out of a book

Keep text large enough to read at a glance. A bookmark is mostly seen as the top inch or two poking out of a book, so put the most interesting part of the design there.

Bookmark ideas for kids

These work in a classroom or at the kitchen table:

  • Animal corner bookmarks: a folded corner with paper ears, eyes and a nose
  • Colour-in strips: printed outlines on card that children colour themselves
  • Name bookmarks: each letter of the child's name in a different colour or pattern
  • Handprint or fingerprint strips, with the prints turned into small animals
  • Monster bookmarks: googly eyes and paper teeth on a card strip
  • Reading-log bookmarks: a strip with boxes to fill in for each book finished

Younger children manage best with a strip that is already cut, so they can go straight to colouring and sticking. Older children can measure and cut their own 2 x 6 strips with a ruler.

How to make bookmarks last

Paper bookmarks get bent and frayed. 4OVER4's advice for printed ones is heavier stock, and it describes lamination as a layer that resists bending and edge wear; at home that translates to:

  • Start on heavier paper: cardstock, cereal-box card or the back of an old greeting card.
  • Cover both sides with clear packing tape, smoothing out bubbles, and trim the edges.
  • Use a laminating pouch if you have a laminator, leaving a narrow clear edge around the strip.
  • Round the corners with scissors or a corner punch so they don't catch and bend.
  • Reinforce the hole with a hole reinforcement sticker before adding a tassel.

Are paper clip bookmarks bad for books?

Paper clip bookmarks are on many idea lists: a button, a charm or a paper flower glued to a large clip. They are fun, but think about which book they go into.

The Library of Congress care advice for paper says not to use paper clips, other fasteners or "dog ear" folding to mark or organise pages. That advice is written for items people want to keep, so it fits a library book, a signed copy or a book you plan to pass on. For those, a flat card, felt or ribbon bookmark is the gentler choice.

Questions people ask

What size is a bookmark?

The most common printed size is 2 x 6 inches (about 51 x 152 mm). 2 x 7 and 2 x 8 inches are the usual larger options, and print services also list a slim 1.5 x 7 and a wide 2.5 x 8.5.

What can I make a bookmark out of?

Cardstock, cereal-box card, scrapbook paper, felt, ribbon, leather offcuts, craft sticks or an envelope corner. Anything thin and stiff enough to stand up between pages works.

What are easy bookmark ideas for kids?

Folded corner bookmarks shaped like animal faces, colour-in strips, handprint or name bookmarks, and collage strips made from magazine pictures. All of them need only paper, scissors, glue and pens.

How do I make a paper bookmark last longer?

Print or draw on heavier card, then cover both sides with clear packing tape or a laminating pouch and trim the edges. A rounded corner punch stops the corners from bending.

Are paper clip bookmarks bad for books?

They can leave marks. The Library of Congress lists paper clips and folding corners to mark pages among the things to avoid on paper you want to keep. For a book you care about, use a flat paper or fabric bookmark.
