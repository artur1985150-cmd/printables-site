  • The fastest tags are cut from scraps you already have: wrapping paper, old cards, cereal boxes, sheet music, maps.
  • Clay, felt, wood and dried citrus take longer but last; they can hang on the tree afterwards.
  • Give each person a colour or symbol, and wrapped gifts sort themselves on Christmas morning.
  • A good tag says who it is for, who it is from, and one short line that fits the person or the gift.

The easiest Christmas gift tags are cut from what you already have: a square of leftover wrapping paper, the front of last year's cards, kraft paper cut into little trees, or a dried orange slice paired with a plain tag and twine. For tags that last, use felt, wood or air-dry clay, which can go on the tree afterwards. Below are ideas sorted by time, a system for telling a family's gifts apart, and wording for every kind of recipient.

Quick paper gift tags you can make tonight

These need scissors, a hole punch and string. Nothing has to dry.

  • Wrapping paper tags. James & Catrin cut tags from one sheet of festive wrapping paper and thread a ribbon through the hole. Glue the paper to card first if it is thin.
  • Old Christmas cards. Cut the picture side into tags; DIYs.com uses a tag punch for neat edges. The back is often blank and ready to write on.
  • Cereal box tags. The grey inside of the box takes marker well. DIYs.com covers them in glitter and buttons.
  • Sheet music and map tags. DecorByAva cuts pages into shapes and adds a red bow, or circles a place on the map that means something to the person.
  • Washi tape stripes. Lay a few strips of patterned tape across a plain white tag and trim the ends. It takes a minute.
  • Kraft tags. Rectangles, stars or little trees cut from brown kraft paper and written on in white gel pen, as James & Catrin do. A stamp is optional.
  • Upcycled business cards. Blank or spare business cards are already tag-sized; DIYs.com stamps them and punches a hole.

Handmade gift tags that last

These take an evening or need drying time, and they are sturdy enough to keep. DecorByAva points out that cardstock, fabric and clay tags can be reused in later seasons.

  • Stamped clay. Roll out air-dry clay, cut circles, stamp a word or name while soft, poke a hole, and let it dry overnight (James & Catrin).
  • Salt dough. Flour, salt and water, cut with cookie cutters, with a hole made by a straw (DIYs.com). Paint it once it is dry.
  • Felt shapes. Cut stockings, mittens or holly leaves from felt with a cookie cutter as the template; write the name in fabric paint.
  • Wood slices. Thin slices of a branch, drilled and sanded, with the name in marker (DIYs.com).
  • Dried citrus. James & Catrin bake thin orange slices on low heat for a few hours until crisp, then pair one with a plain tag, twine and a sprig of eucalyptus, rosemary or pine.
  • Yarn tassels. Wrap yarn about 50 times around a piece of cardboard, tie the top and cut the bottom loops. James & Catrin put the whole tag, tassel included, at about ten minutes.
  • Button snowmen. Three buttons in three sizes glued on a tag, with arms drawn in pen.

A colour code for a family's gifts

Under one tree with a dozen people, reading every tag on Christmas morning is slow, and children count their pile days before. A simple fix, our own:

  1. Give each person one colour: red for one child, green for another, gold for a grandparent.
  2. Use that colour for the tag, the ribbon or the paper, whichever is easiest.
  3. Add a symbol for families with more people than colours: star, snowflake, tree, bell.
  4. Keep the key with an adult. Names can still go on the back.

The same idea helps a Secret Santa table or a classroom swap: number the tags, and keep a list of who has which number.

Gifts "from Santa" are a classic giveaway. Use a paper and a pen you never use for your own gifts, and keep them away from the rest of the wrapping supplies.

What to write on a Christmas gift tag

The basics are "To" and "From" with names. One more line makes the tag worth reading. Here is wording we wrote, by recipient:

For Tag line
Your partner "Still my favourite person to wake up with on Christmas morning."
A child "Checked twice. Definitely on the nice list."
A teenager "No, it's not socks. Well, not only socks."
Parents "For all the Christmases you made happen. Your turn to sit down."
Grandparents "Made with sticky fingers and a lot of love."
A teacher "Thank you for a great first half of the year."
A neighbour "For the best neighbours on the street. Happy Christmas!"
A mail carrier or delivery driver "For all the parcels this year. Warm wishes."
A coworker "Something to get you through January."
A host "Thank you for having us. Merry Christmas."
A friend far away "Wish we could open this together."
A pet sitter or babysitter "For looking after the people and places we love."

Gift tags for themed gifts

DIYs.com includes a printable set for the "want, need, make, read" idea: four tags for four gifts, one for something the child wants, one they need, one to make and one to read. It keeps a gift list short and gives each present a reason. You can make the same set by hand with four colours.

For gifts that are an experience rather than a box, the tag can carry the gift itself: "Good for one cinema trip with me." Our coupon book ideas work as a set of tags tied together.

Turning a gift tag into an ornament

A sturdy tag can stay with the gift. Add a loop of ribbon or string long enough to hang on a branch, and write the year on the back. Clay, felt, wood and dried orange tags hang best. A child's handmade tag with their name and the year becomes a decoration that comes out every December.

Questions people ask

What can I use to make Christmas gift tags?

Almost anything flat that takes ink: cardstock, kraft paper, leftover wrapping paper, last year's Christmas cards, sheet music, maps, felt, air-dry or salt dough clay, wood slices or dried orange slices. Punch a hole and tie it on with twine or ribbon.

What do you write on a Christmas gift tag?

At minimum, 'To' and 'From' with names. Add a short line that fits the person or the gift, such as 'For the best snowball thrower on the street' or 'Open first, you'll need it today.' Keep it to one or two lines so it fits.

How do you make Christmas gift tags from clay?

Roll out air-dry clay, cut shapes with a cookie cutter, press a name or word in with letter stamps while it is soft, and poke a hole at the top. James & Catrin let stamped clay tags dry overnight, then thread them with twine. Paint them gold or silver if you like.

How do you label gifts so kids can't read the names?

Use a code instead of names: each child gets a colour of paper, a ribbon colour or a symbol such as a star or a snowflake. Only the adults know the key, so nobody can count their pile in advance.
