In short

  • A pixel has no fixed size in centimetres. Centimetres = pixels ÷ dpi × 2.54, so the answer depends on the dpi.
  • At 96 dpi, the figure CSS is built on, 1 pixel is 0.0265 cm and 100 pixels are 2.65 cm.
  • For a print, use the printer's dpi: at 300 dpi 100 pixels cover 0.85 cm and 1 cm takes 118 pixels.
  • Going the other way, pixels = cm ÷ 2.54 × dpi: a 10 x 15 cm print at 300 dpi needs 1181 x 1772 pixels.

To turn pixels into cm, divide the pixels by the dpi and multiply by 2.54. At 96 dpi, the figure web pages are built on, 100 pixels are 2.65 cm and 1 pixel is 0.0265 cm. At 300 dpi, the usual figure for a sharp print, the same 100 pixels cover only 0.85 cm. The chart below shows both, with 72 and 150 dpi next to them, and the converter works in either direction.

The pixels to cm formula

A pixel is a count, not a length. It becomes a length when you decide how many pixels sit on one inch, and that number is the dpi. One inch is exactly 25.4 mm (NIST), which is 2.54 cm. So:

  • centimetres = pixels ÷ dpi × 2.54
  • pixels = centimetres ÷ 2.54 × dpi

Take 1000 pixels. At 96 dpi that is 1000 ÷ 96 × 2.54 = 26.46 cm. At 300 dpi it is 1000 ÷ 300 × 2.54 = 8.47 cm. Nothing about the image changed between the two sums. Only the density did.

The chart rounds centimetres to two decimals. That is why its first row looks coarse: 1 pixel at 96 dpi is 0.0265 cm, shown as 0.03.

Why most converters say 1 pixel is 0.0265 cm

Type "pixels to cm" into a search engine and nearly every converter gives the same answer, 0.0264583 cm for one pixel. They all assume 96 pixels per inch.

That number comes from the web. The W3C specification for CSS units fixes the ratio: 1 inch = 2.54 cm = 96 px, and 1 px is 1/96 of an inch. A web designer who writes a width of 378 px and one who writes 10 cm are asking the browser for almost exactly the same thing.

The same specification says the number is a convention. For screens it recommends tying the px unit to a "reference pixel" and notes that the physical units then might not match their physical measurements. Its authors explain why the ratio was fixed anyway: too much existing content assumed 96 dpi.

So 96 is the right column for CSS, for a web layout, or when a form asks for a size "at 96 dpi". It tells you little about paper, and little about a phone.

Pixels to cm for printing: use the printer's dpi

When the pixels are going onto paper, the dpi is whatever the print is made at. Printful, which prints posters to order, strongly recommends files at 300 DPI, sets its own Design Maker to 150 DPI and accepts 75 DPI as the minimum for paper products.

A printable you bought does not have one "size in cm" either. A 3000 x 4000 pixel file is a 25 x 34 cm print at 300 dpi and a 51 x 68 cm print at 150 dpi; the larger one is softer up close. If the listing gives pixels only, the 300 column tells you the largest size that still looks crisp. The frame sizes those centimetres match are in our wall art sizes guide.

Cm to pixels: how big to make a file

The second mode of the converter runs the sum backwards. Use it when you know the print size and need the canvas.

Centimetres rarely land on a whole pixel. 10 cm at 300 dpi is 1181.1 pixels, so the converter shows the unrounded figure and then rounds to the nearest whole pixel. One pixel either way is a twelfth of a millimetre at that density, far below what a trimmer or a frame mat hides.

Set the canvas in pixels before you start drawing. Enlarging a small file afterwards adds pixels without adding detail. The printer settings that keep the size true on paper, such as printing at 100% and not "fit to page", are in our guide on how to print wall art.

On a screen, a pixel is not 0.0265 cm

It is tempting to hold a ruler to the phone and expect 378 pixels to measure 10 cm. They will not, because screens pack pixels far more tightly than 96 to the inch.

Apple's specifications for the iPhone 17 give 2622-by-1206 pixels at 460 ppi. Run the formula with the real density and the screen's width is 1206 ÷ 460 × 2.54 = 6.66 cm. Run it at 96 dpi and the same 1206 pixels come out as 31.91 cm, wider than a sheet of A4 is tall.

So for a wallpaper or any picture that only ever lives on a screen, centimetres are the wrong question. Match the screen's pixel count and stop there. The counts for current iPhone, Pixel and Galaxy models are in our phone wallpaper size table.

Which column is yours

  • Laying out a web page or an email, or filling in a form that says "96 dpi": the 96 column.
  • Printing at home or sending a file to a print shop: the 300 column for work seen up close, 150 for a large poster seen from across the room if the file cannot do better.
  • Checking how little paper a small picture really covers: the 72 column. It sits just under Printful's 75 DPI minimum for paper products.
  • Making a phone or desktop background: no column. Use the screen's size in pixels.

A word on names. Strictly, an image has pixels per inch (ppi) and a printer lays down dots per inch. Printful and most print shops say DPI for both, and this page does the same.

How the numbers here are rounded

The inch is 25.4 mm exactly, as NIST defines it, so every figure on this page starts from 2.54 cm. Centimetres are rounded to two decimals in the chart and in the converter. Pixels in the reverse mode are rounded to the nearest whole pixel, with the unrounded figure printed next to them so you can round the other way if a template asks for it. The four dpi columns are the same ones as in our A4 chart, plus 96 for the web.

Questions people ask

How many cm is 1 pixel?

At 96 dpi, 0.0265 cm (2.54 ÷ 96). At 300 dpi it is 0.0085 cm, at 150 dpi 0.0169 cm and at 72 dpi 0.0353 cm. A pixel has no size of its own; you get centimetres only after choosing how many pixels go on an inch.

How many pixels are in 1 cm?

37.8 at 96 dpi, 118.1 at 300 dpi, 59.1 at 150 dpi and 28.3 at 72 dpi. The rule is pixels = cm ÷ 2.54 × dpi.

How do I convert pixels to cm for printing?

Divide the pixels by the dpi you will print at, then multiply by 2.54. A 3000 x 4000 pixel image at 300 dpi prints 25.4 x 33.87 cm. Printful, which prints posters to order, strongly recommends files at 300 DPI.

Why do converters say 100 pixels is 2.65 cm?

They assume 96 pixels per inch. That is the figure in the W3C definition of CSS units, where 1 inch equals 96 px. It describes how web pages are laid out, not how large a picture prints or how big it looks on a given phone.

How many cm is 1920 x 1080 pixels?

At 96 dpi, 50.8 x 28.58 cm. Printed at 300 dpi the same image is 16.26 x 9.14 cm.
