In short

  • A4 is 210 x 297 mm, so it is 2480 x 3508 pixels at 300 dpi, 1240 x 1754 at 150 dpi and 595 x 842 at 72 dpi.
  • The rule: pixels = millimetres ÷ 25.4 × dpi, rounded to the nearest whole pixel. One inch is exactly 25.4 mm.
  • US Letter at 300 dpi is 2550 x 3300: wider and shorter than A4, so a file made for one does not fill the other exactly.
  • Going the other way, divide pixels by dpi: a 3000 x 4000 image covers A4 at 300 dpi and A3 at 150 dpi.

A4 in pixels is 2480 x 3508 at 300 dpi, 1240 x 1754 at 150 dpi and 595 x 842 at 72 dpi, portrait. A4 measures 210 x 297 mm, and each side becomes pixels as millimetres ÷ 25.4 × dpi, rounded to the nearest whole pixel. How the pixels are worked out

A pixel has no size of its own. It gets one when you decide how many of them go on an inch of paper, and that number is the dpi. So the sum always runs through inches:

  1. Turn the paper size into inches. One inch is exactly 25.4 mm (NIST), so A4's 210 mm is 8.27 inches and its 297 mm is 11.69 inches.
  2. Multiply each side by the dpi. At 300 dpi that gives 2480.3 and 3507.9.
  3. Round to a whole pixel. We round to the nearest one, which gives 2480 x 3508.

Sizes that are already in inches skip the first step. US Letter is 8.5 x 11 inches, so at 300 dpi it is 2550 x 3300 with nothing to round.

Round up every time and A4's short side becomes 2481; round down every time and its long side becomes 3507. Start from inches already cut to 8.3 x 11.7 and you get 2490 x 3510. The differences are a few pixels on sides of thousands, too small to see in print. We picked one rule, the nearest whole pixel, and use it everywhere on the page: in the chart and in the calculator.

Which dpi to choose: 72, 150 or 300

The same sheet of A4 can be 595 pixels wide or 2480. What changes is how many pixels the printer gets for every inch, and so how fine the detail can be.

  • 300 dpi is the number to aim for when the print should look crisp up close. Printful, which prints posters to order, strongly recommends 300 DPI files.
  • 150 dpi is half that, a middle step. Printful's own Design Maker is set to 150 DPI.
  • 72 dpi is in the chart so you can see how little paper a small image really covers. Printful's minimum for paper products is 75 DPI, so 72 sits just under the floor of one big print-on-demand shop.

A word on names. Strictly, a file has pixels per inch (ppi) and a printer lays down dots per inch. Printful and most print shops say DPI for both, and so does this page.

A4, A5 and A3: why the numbers repeat

Look down the chart and the same figures turn up in different rows. A5 at 300 dpi is 1748 x 2480, and 2480 is A4's short side. A3 at 300 dpi is 3508 x 4961, and 3508 is A4's long side.

That is because each A size is the one above cut in half across its long side. Canon's measurements show it: A5 is 148 x 210 mm, A4 is 210 x 297 mm, A3 is 297 x 420 mm. The shape stays the same at every size, so an A4 file scales to A5 or A3 without cropping. It just carries more or fewer pixels per inch once printed. US Letter is not A4

In the United States the everyday sheet is US Letter, 8.5 x 11 inches; most other countries use A4, which HP describes as slightly narrower and taller. In pixels at 300 dpi:

Width Height
A4 2480 3508
US Letter 2550 3300
Difference Letter is 70 wider Letter is 208 shorter

The shapes differ too: A4's long side is 1.41 times its short side, Letter's 1.29 times. An A4 file printed on Letter with "fit to page" shrinks to about 94% and leaves white strips down both sides. Printed at full size, it loses a strip at the bottom. If you design a printable for buyers in both places, make one file per sheet. From pixels to print size

The calculator's second mode answers the question buyers of printables ask most: is this file big enough? Divide each side in pixels by the dpi to get inches, then compare with the paper.

Take an image of 3000 x 4000 pixels:

  • At 300 dpi it is 10 x 13.33 inches. That covers A4 (2480 x 3508), US Letter, A5, 8x10, 5x7 and 4x6, but not A3.
  • At 150 dpi it is 20 x 26.67 inches, enough for A3 (1754 x 2480 at that density).

A file that covers a size in one direction also covers it turned sideways, as long as the shape matches. A 3508 x 2480 landscape image is the same A4 at 300 dpi. If the shapes do not match, part of the picture is cropped or a white margin appears; the gallery wall ideas keep every frame in one shape family for that reason.

Photo sizes: 4x6, 5x7 and 8x10

Photo sizes are defined in inches, so their pixel counts come out round. At 300 dpi a 4x6 photo is 1200 x 1800, a 5x7 is 1500 x 2100 and an 8x10 is 2400 x 3000. Canon's printer specifications list them as 101.6 x 152.4 mm, 127.0 x 177.8 mm and 203.2 x 254.0 mm.

An 8x10 at 300 dpi needs fewer pixels than A4 at 300 dpi, 2400 x 3000 against 2480 x 3508, so any A4-ready file has the pixels for a sharp 8x10. The shapes differ, though: fitted to the 8-inch width, the A4 picture runs about 13% too tall, and that strip is cropped from the top or bottom.

Making a file at the right size

When you start a design from scratch, set the canvas to the pixels in the chart for the largest paper and dpi you will need, then work at that size. Scaling a small file up later adds pixels but no detail.

A few uses of the chart beyond wall art:

  • Sticker sheets are laid out on Letter or A4 paper, so the canvas for a full page of homemade stickers is 2550 x 3300 or 2480 x 3508 at 300 dpi.
  • A sublimation design is also printed on Letter or A4; the tumbler wrap size chart gives the wrap sizes to place on that sheet.
  • Screens work the other way: the pixels are fixed by the device and inches do not matter. For those, see phone wallpaper size and Twitch overlay size.

How we rounded and where the sizes come from

Paper sizes are taken from Canon's printer specifications and checked against HP's paper guide and Epson's paper specifications, which give the same millimetres. The inch is 25.4 mm exactly, as defined by NIST. In the chart and the calculator, pixels are rounded to the nearest whole pixel. In the reverse mode, inches are rounded to two decimals and millimetres to one, while the "big enough for" list compares your pixels with the chart's own figures, so a 2480 x 3508 file always counts as A4 at 300 dpi.

Questions people ask

What is A4 size in pixels at 300 dpi?

2480 x 3508 pixels in portrait, 3508 x 2480 in landscape. A4 is 210 x 297 mm; 210 ÷ 25.4 × 300 = 2480.3 and 297 ÷ 25.4 × 300 = 3507.9, rounded to the nearest whole pixel.

What is A4 in pixels at 72 dpi?

595 x 842 pixels. At 150 dpi it is 1240 x 1754. The paper does not change, only how many pixels you put on each inch of it.

What resolution should an A4 printable be?

For a sharp print, 2480 x 3508 pixels or more. Printful, which prints posters to order, strongly recommends files at 300 DPI and accepts 75 DPI as the minimum for paper products, so a smaller file still prints, only softer.

Is A4 the same as US Letter in pixels?

No. At 300 dpi A4 is 2480 x 3508 and US Letter is 2550 x 3300. Letter is 70 pixels wider and 208 pixels shorter, and the two have different shapes.

How big can I print a 1920 x 1080 image?

At 300 dpi, 6.4 x 3.6 inches (162.6 x 91.4 mm): too small for a 4x6 photo, which needs 1200 x 1800. At 150 dpi it reaches 12.8 x 7.2 inches and covers a 5x7 photo turned sideways, but not A4.
