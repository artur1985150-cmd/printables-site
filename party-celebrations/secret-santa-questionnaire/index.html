What is a Secret Santa questionnaire?

In Secret Santa, each person in a group is given someone else at random and buys that person a gift. The giver may stay secret or be revealed at the end. In Ireland the same game is called Kris Kindle, and in Germany Wichteln.

The questionnaire fixes the hard part. At work or in a big family you often draw someone you barely know. Five honest answers turn a guess into a gift they will use.

It is a different game from a gift swap where people open or steal presents. That one has its own page: white elephant rules.

Secret Santa questions to ask

Start with these. They work for any group.

  1. What is something you'd love but would never buy for yourself?
  2. Is there anything you don't want? (Mugs, candles, scented things, novelty socks.)
  3. Any allergies or foods you avoid?
  4. Favourite snack or sweet?
  5. Coffee, tea, hot chocolate, or none?
  6. Favourite colour?
  7. What do you do on a free afternoon?
  8. A shop or a gift card you'd actually use?
  9. Something you collect, or a fandom you love?
  10. Something you use every day and keep running out of?
  11. Sweet or salty?
  12. Homemade or bought?

The first two questions do the most work. 101 Planners names five that carry a form: what the person would love, what they would hate, allergies or dietary restrictions, favourite snack, and a gift card they'd use.

Secret Santa questionnaire for work

A work form should be quick to fill in at a desk and safe to read by anyone. 101 Planners says a family exchange can ask for sizes and an office one should not, and suggests putting the budget and the return date on the form.

  • Favourite snack, and anything you can't eat?
  • Coffee order or favourite tea?
  • What would make your desk nicer: a plant, a notebook, a good pen, a mug?
  • Favourite café or lunch place nearby?
  • A hobby outside work?
  • Book, podcast or film you've liked this year?
  • One thing please don't buy me.

Skip sizes, body care, money, religion and relationships. If you are planning the party itself, our Christmas party games cover the rest of the afternoon.

Secret Santa questions for family and kids

Families can ask more, because the giver will see the person again at dinner.

  • Sizes: T-shirt, jumper, socks, gloves.
  • A short wish list with three items at different prices under the limit.
  • Favourite book series or author.
  • Board game or card game you'd play with us.

For children, 101 Planners suggests fewer questions and more room to write. We would keep it to five or six. Ask about favourite characters, toys, games, colours and sweets, and add one allergy line for a parent to fill in. A child who can't write yet can draw the answers.

Fun Secret Santa questions (this or that)

A few quick ticks make the form lighter and still tell the giver a lot. Purely Printables builds a this-or-that section into its form.

  • Sweet or salty?
  • Coffee or tea?
  • Book or film?
  • Cosy night in or day out?
  • Practical or fun?
  • Handmade or shop-bought?
  • Chocolate or sweets?
  • Board game or puzzle?

The same pairs make a quick round at the party itself.

What each question tells the gift giver

This is the part the printable forms leave out. Each answer rules gifts in or out.

How to read a vague answer

Some people write "anything is fine". Then you have to work from the answers they did give.

  • Combine two answers. Tea plus "reads on the train" points to a travel mug and a tea sampler.
  • Use the "don't want" line as your first filter.
  • When food is the only clear answer, buy a good version of the snack they named.
  • When a gift card is the only clear answer, add a small treat and a handwritten note so it doesn't feel like a voucher in an envelope.
  • Ask the organiser to pass a question on to them without saying who's asking.

A card or tag finishes it off. Our Christmas card messages have short lines that fit a tag.

How to hand out and collect the forms

The draw only stays secret if one person handles the forms.

  1. Set the date and the spending limit, and write both on the form.
  2. Send the form with a hand-in date a week before the draw.
  3. Players return forms to the organiser, folded or by private message, with their name on top.
  4. Do the draw.
  5. The organiser gives each giver only the form of the person they drew.
  6. Send a reminder a few days before the exchange.

Simplify Create Inspire plans an exchange in the same order: date and budget, sign-up and survey, name draw, the event, and reminders. If the organiser takes part too, ask someone else to hand over the organiser's own form.

Questions people ask

What questions should be on a Secret Santa questionnaire?

Something you'd love but wouldn't buy yourself, something you don't want, allergies or foods you avoid, a favourite snack or drink, a favourite colour, a hobby, a shop or gift card you'd use, and coffee or tea. Add sizes only for family exchanges.

How many questions should a Secret Santa form have?

We suggest eight to twelve for most groups. Five is the least that still gives the giver a real lead, and 101 Planners advises keeping a work form under fifteen.

What should you not ask on a work Secret Santa form?

Clothing sizes, anything about the body or health beyond allergies, money, religion and relationships. Keep it to snacks, drinks, desk items, hobbies, shops and gift cards.

What is a Secret Santa called in Ireland?

Kris Kindle (sometimes spelled Kris Kringel). Canada and Australia use Secret Santa or Kris Kringle, and Germany calls it Wichteln.
