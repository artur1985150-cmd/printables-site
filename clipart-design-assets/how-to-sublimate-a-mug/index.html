In short

  • You need a poly-coated sublimation mug, a sublimation printer and ink, sublimation paper and heat-resistant tape.
  • Mirror the design, trim it close, tape it tight to the mug with the printed side against it, then press.
  • Settings come from the blank and press makers: HPN lists 385°F for 190 seconds for its 11 and 15 oz mugs in its own press; the Cricut Mug Press sets itself and averages 6 minutes.
  • Wrap sizes from makers: 11 oz about 8.2 x 3.7 in, 15 oz about 8.6 x 4.3 in; keep the design at least 0.5 in from the handle.
  • Let the mug cool 15 to 30 minutes before you touch it, and work in a ventilated room.

To sublimate a mug, print a mirrored design on sublimation paper with sublimation ink, tape it tightly around a poly-coated sublimation mug with the printed side against it, and heat it in a mug press or oven. Time and temperature come from the maker of your blank and press: HeatPressNation lists 385°F for 190 seconds for its 11 oz mug in its own press, while the Cricut Mug Press sets itself.

Below are the steps, the settings makers publish for their own blanks, and the wrap sizes. We have no blanks of our own to test with, so every number here is taken from a maker's page, linked in the sources.

What you need to sublimate a mug

  • A sublimation mug: a blank with a poly coating. Cricut's FAQ tells you to look for poly-coated, sublimation-compatible products, so buy mugs sold as sublimation blanks rather than ordinary kitchen mugs.
  • A sublimation printer with sublimation ink.
  • Sublimation paper.
  • Heat-resistant tape.
  • A mug press, a press with a mug attachment, or an oven with a mug wrap or shrink sleeve, depending on what your blank's maker lists.
  • A lint roller or lint-free cloth, butcher paper if your press calls for it, and heat-resistant gloves.

The design itself can be your own lettering, a photo or licensed clipart. If you plan to sell the mugs, the clipart licence has to allow products for sale.

How to sublimate a mug, step by step

  1. Size the design to the mug's print area (see the size table below).
  2. Mirror the design before printing, as Cricut's paper instructions tell you to.
  3. Print on the printable side of the sublimation paper and let the ink dry; Cricut gives about 1 minute.
  4. Trim the paper close to the design so there is little excess.
  5. Clean the mug with a lint roller or a lint-free cloth. Cricut notes that tiny fibres can show up in the transfer.
  6. Wrap the design around the mug, printed side against the mug, with no gaps between paper and surface. Keep it on the smooth, straight part and at least 0.5 in from the handle.
  7. Tape the edges down with heat-resistant tape. If your press or method calls for butcher paper, cover the design with it and tape that too.
  8. Preheat the press and press the mug at the settings for your blank.
  9. Lift the mug out by the handle and set it on a heat-safe mat. Let it cool before you peel off the tape and paper.

How long to sublimate a mug

There is no single answer, because each blank and press has its own settings. Here is what makers publish for their own products:

HeatPressNation adds that actual settings may vary with the press, the artwork and other factors. Cricut says its press time varies with room and mug temperature, and that the press has no manual time or temperature settings.

If your blank came with instructions, use those. If it did not, ask the seller before you guess: a wrong setting wastes a mug.

How to sublimate a mug with a heat press or mug press

A mug press closes a curved heating element around the mug. A few things make the difference:

  • Check that the mug fits. Cricut's Mug Press takes mugs from 10 to 16 oz with a diameter of 3.2 to 3.4 in and a maximum height of 4.72 in, with a straight pressing area. HeatPressNation's CraftPro press comes with attachments for 11 to 15 oz mugs and 12 to 30 oz skinny tumblers.
  • Set the pressure the way your press manual says.
  • Push the mug all the way in. Cricut warns that a mug left hovering over the base heater when you close the lever can damage the press.
  • Make sure the heating element covers the whole design.

For tumblers in the same press, the wrap is a different size. Our tumbler wrap size chart lists makers' print areas.

How to measure a mug for sublimation

A mug wrap is shorter than the mug's circumference because the handle takes up part of it. That is why makers list print areas rather than diameters:

To measure a mug without a published print area, measure the height of the straight band, then run a tape around the body from 0.5 in past one side of the handle to 0.5 in before the other. Print a test on plain paper and wrap it before you use sublimation paper.

How to sublimate a coffee mug in an oven

Ovens can press several mugs at once but need a mug wrap or shrink sleeve to hold the paper tight. HeatPressNation lists 400 to 425°F for 10 to 18 minutes for its 20 oz jumbo mug, with a mug wrap required. Follow the instructions of the wrap and blank makers, and handle the hot mug and wrap with heat-resistant gloves.

Safety when you sublimate mugs

  • The press gets very hot. Cricut warns that its machine operates at temperatures that can cause burns; the same goes for any mug press or oven.
  • Work in a well-ventilated room. Cricut says pressing produces odours and vapours.
  • Let the mug cool for 15 to 30 minutes before handling, which is Cricut's figure for its press.
  • Food contact: Cricut says its own blanks are safe to drink from once pressed and fully cool. For other brands, it says the coating maker must test and certify that the combination is safe for mouth contact. Buy blanks whose maker states this.
  • Follow the care instructions for your blank. Makers list dishwasher and microwave safety per product.

Questions people ask

How long do you sublimate a mug?

As long as the maker of your blank and press says. HeatPressNation lists 385°F for 190 seconds for its 11 oz and 15 oz ceramic mugs in its own mug press. The Cricut Mug Press has no manual settings and takes about 6 minutes on average. Ovens take longer: HPN lists 10 to 18 minutes at 400 to 425°F for its 20 oz mug in a mug wrap.

Can you sublimate any mug?

No. Cricut's Mug Press FAQ tells you to look for poly-coated, sublimation-compatible blanks, and blank makers sell mugs with that coating for this purpose. An ordinary kitchen mug is not sold as one, so buy blanks labelled for sublimation.

Is a sublimated mug safe to drink from?

Cricut says its own blanks are safe for drinking once pressed and fully cooled. For other brands it says the coating's maker has to test and certify that the combination is safe for mouth contact. Buy blanks whose maker states this, and follow their care instructions.

What size should a mug sublimation design be?

Use the print area for your blank. HeatPressNation lists 8.2 x 3.7 in for its 11 oz mug and 8.6 x 4.3 in for its 15 oz. Cricut gives a maximum of 8.75 in long and 3.79 in high for its 12 oz mug and 4.25 in high for its 15 oz, with at least 0.5 in clear of the handle.

Why is my sublimated mug faded or blurry?

Common causes are paper that was not tight against the mug, design placed on curves or too close to the handle, lint or dust on the surface, or the wrong time and temperature for that blank. Cricut lists lint-rolling, a flat design and trimming the paper close as its tips for clean transfers.
