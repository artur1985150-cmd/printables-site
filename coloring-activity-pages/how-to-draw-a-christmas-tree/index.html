In short

  • Every method starts the same way: a tall triangle for the shape and a short rectangle for the trunk.
  • The easiest tree is three triangles stacked, smallest on top; small children can manage it.
  • For a fuller tree, draw tiers with scalloped or jagged edges that reach past the guide triangle.
  • Real species look different: Fraser fir branches turn slightly upward, white pine needles are long and soft, blue spruce is bluish-grey.
  • Decorate in this order: garland lines, then baubles where the garland dips, then the star and presents.

To draw a Christmas tree, sketch a tall triangle with a short rectangle trunk under it, then draw the branches in layers that get wider towards the bottom, letting their edges stick out past the triangle. Add a star on top, a few curved lines for garland and circles for baubles. Below are four ways to draw it, from three stacked triangles to a realistic fir, and how to draw the species you actually have.

What you need

A pencil, an eraser, paper and something to colour with. Easy Drawing Guides lists a pencil, eraser, an optional black marker and coloured pencils or crayons for its cartoon tree, and puts it at about 15 minutes. Art Projects for Kids uses drawing paper, a pencil, a fine black marker and markers or crayons, and says its fuller scene takes 45 minutes.

One trick from Art Projects for Kids helps with every method: fold the paper in half lengthways and widthways, crease it, and open it out. The vertical crease is the tree's centre line, and the creases fade by the time you finish.

Method 1: three stacked triangles (easiest)

Good for young children and quick doodles on gift tags.

  1. Draw a small triangle near the top of the page.
  2. Draw a wider triangle below it, with its point tucked under the first one.
  3. Draw a third, widest triangle below that.
  4. Add a small rectangle trunk under the middle.
  5. Put a star on the top point.
  6. Draw a wavy line across each triangle for garland and dots for baubles.

Rub out the parts of each triangle's point that sit behind the triangle above, and the tree reads as layers instead of a stack of party hats.

Method 2: a cartoon tree with scalloped tiers

This is the style of Easy Drawing Guides' tutorial.

  1. Draw a five-pointed star at the top.
  2. Under it, draw an upside-down V for the top tier.
  3. Close the bottom of the V with three joined curves, like a scalloped hem.
  4. Draw the next tier below: two lines going out wider, then another scalloped edge.
  5. Add a third tier the same way, wider again.
  6. Draw a trunk: two short lines joined by a curve.
  7. Drape pairs of curved lines across each tier for tinsel or ribbon.
  8. Add round baubles, and a candy cane hooked over one ribbon if you like.
  9. Erase any stray candy cane lines, fill the rest of the tree with baubles of different sizes, then colour.

Easy Drawing Guides suggests red and green, blue on white, or a mix of colours for the decorations.

Method 3: a layered tree in a snowy scene

Art Projects for Kids builds a whole picture around the tree. The steps, in short:

  1. Draw a star at the centre top and two tree sections below it.
  2. Add three more sections and a snow line at the bottom, so the trunk stays hidden in snow.
  3. Draw simple lines for garland, then circles for ornaments.
  4. Draw a horizon line, two small trees in the background and mountains behind.
  5. Add presents and candy in front.
  6. Trace the final lines in black, colour, and add shadows.

Its tips: don't outline the snow in pencil or black; draw the snow lines in sky blue after the rest so the background stays soft. Layer an extra shade of green on the right side of the tree for depth.

Method 4: a realistic tree

A realistic tree has almost no outline. You build it from strokes.

  1. Draw a light guide triangle and a centre line. Keep the pencil soft; these lines will disappear.
  2. Mark four or five tiers with faint horizontal lines, closer together near the top.
  3. At each tier, draw short strokes going down and outward from the centre line, like the needles hanging off a branch.
  4. Let some branch tips poke past the guide triangle and others stop short. Real trees are uneven.
  5. Leave darker gaps between the tiers, where you see into the tree.
  6. Shade the inside and one side darker. Keep the lit side lighter.
  7. Add lights as small dots with a light halo and baubles as circles with a white shine mark.

How to draw different kinds of Christmas trees

Most drawings show the same green cone. The National Christmas Tree Association's species pages describe how the common trees differ, so you can draw the one you actually have.

Tree What the NCTA says How to draw it
Fraser fir Uniformly pyramid-shaped; strong branches turned slightly upward, compact look; dark blue-green Neat triangle, branch strokes curving up at the tips
Noble fir Needles twist upward, exposing the lower branches; stiff branches for heavy ornaments; bluish-green, looks silver Clear tiers with visible branches underneath, a silvery highlight
Scotch pine Needles in bundles of two, about 1 to 3 inches; stiff branches Spiky clusters, slightly irregular tiers
White pine Soft, flexible needles 2½ to 5 inches long in bundles of five; bluish-green; not recommended for heavy ornaments Long, soft drooping strokes, light ornaments only
Colorado blue spruce Narrow, pyramidal shape; sharp needles; dull bluish-grey to silvery blue Slim cone, short sharp strokes, blue-grey colouring

So a Fraser fir drawing has tidy layers with tips that lift, while a white pine looks shaggy and soft. If your tree is artificial, look at its branch tips and needle length and pick the row that comes closest.

Decorating your Christmas tree drawing

Decorate in this order and nothing ends up floating:

  1. Garland first: curved lines that dip between two points on each tier.
  2. Baubles where the garland dips, plus a few hanging from branch tips.
  3. Lights as dots along the garland or scattered evenly.
  4. The star or an angel on top.
  5. Presents under the tree, drawn as boxes with a cross of ribbon.

Turning the drawing into a colouring page

Trace a Method 2 or Method 3 tree in black pen, let it dry, rub out the pencil and photocopy it before you colour. The copy is a colouring page for a younger child. With round baubles drawn a little larger, the same page works with dot markers; our dot marker activities explain how big the circles need to be. The same tree also fits on the walls of a gingerbread house piped in green icing.

Questions people ask

What is the easiest way to draw a Christmas tree?

Draw three triangles stacked on top of each other, smallest at the top, each one overlapping the one above. Add a small rectangle trunk and a star. It takes a couple of minutes and works for young children.

How do you draw a Christmas tree step by step for kids?

Fold the paper in half both ways for centring creases, draw the star at the top, then the tree in layers getting wider towards the bottom, then garland lines and circles for ornaments. Art Projects for Kids adds a snowy ground, small background trees and presents to make a scene.

How do you make a Christmas tree drawing look realistic?

Skip the outline. Draw a light guide triangle, then build the tree from short downward strokes in rows, leaving gaps between tiers so the darker inside shows. Make one side darker than the other and let a few branch tips break the triangle.

What colours do I use for a Christmas tree drawing?

Two or three greens: a dark one for the inside and shadow side, a mid green for most branches, a lighter one for tips on the lit side. For a blue spruce, mix in blue-grey; the National Christmas Tree Association describes its needles as dull bluish-grey to silvery blue.
