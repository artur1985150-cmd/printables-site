In short

  • Pick the build first: a kit, a house baked from scratch, a no-bake graham cracker house or a paper house. The ideas that work depend on it.
  • King Arthur Baking says the walls need 4 to 12 hours before the roof goes on, and each roof panel at least an hour.
  • Plan the design on paper: colour a printed house outline, then copy it in candy so the icing goes on once.
  • Easy themes for kids: candy cottage, sprinkle house, peppermint house. Harder ones: log cabin, stained-glass church, a whole village.
  • Stained-glass windows are hard candy pieces melted in the oven; put them in before the roof.

The best gingerbread house ideas start with how you build it. A kit suits candy cottages and sprinkle houses, a scratch-baked house lets you cut windows and make a log cabin or a stained-glass church, and a graham cracker house is the quick no-bake option for kids. Below are 30 designs sorted by build type, roof and wall ideas, and a two-day plan with the drying times.

First choose how you will build it

Every idea below fits one of four builds. Choosing first saves you from planning a chalet with cut-out windows on a kit that has no windows.

Easy gingerbread house ideas for kids

These work on a kit or a graham cracker house and need no piping skills.

  1. Candy cottage: gumdrops on the roof line, a candy cane on each corner. Taste of Home's Christmas cottage uses gumdrops.
  2. Sprinkle house: cover each wall in icing and press it into a plate of sprinkles. Wilton has two versions of this.
  3. Peppermint house: round peppermints in rows on the roof and red and white icing on the walls.
  4. Rainbow roof: one row of candy per colour, top to bottom.
  5. Snowy house: white icing only, dripped off the eaves for icicles, with coarse sugar on the ground.
  6. Holly berry house: holly sprinkles and gumdrops do most of the decorating; Wilton calls its version a beginner project.
  7. Pretzel fence: mini pretzels stuck in a line of icing around the base.
  8. Candy train: Taste of Home makes the boxcars from gingerbread or graham crackers and fills them with candy.
  9. Marshmallow snowmen in the yard: a few marshmallows on a skewer with piped eyes and nose, as on Taste of Home's list.
  10. Ice cream cone trees: King Arthur Baking covers sugar cones in flattened green gumdrops.

For a party, the Chinet blog puts each house on its own plate and sorts candy into compartment trays so children can see everything at once. One writer there hot-glued the walls ahead of time so small hands couldn't break them; that house is no longer food.

Gingerbread house ideas from scratch

Baking your own panels lets you change the shape and cut openings.

  1. Log cabin: pretzel rods laid along the walls. Taste of Home adds shredded wheat and candy rocks.
  2. Ski lodge: candy rocks for the walls, rolled wafer cookies as beams, royal icing and coarse sugar on the roof (Taste of Home).
  3. Stained-glass church: tall windows filled with melted hard candy (see the section below).
  4. Barn: red icing walls, white trim in an X on the doors.
  5. Monochrome chalet: a white-only palette, which Taste of Home calls a more modern twist.
  6. Brick cottage: small rectangles of icing piped in rows, or chocolate pieces laid as bricks.
  7. Painted house: cover the walls in white fondant and draw on them, as in one of Wilton's houses, which uses edible markers.
  8. Written walls: pipe words such as "Joy" or a family name along the side. Wilton suggests outlining the words with edible markers first, which makes piping easier.
  9. Knitted house: icing piped in rows of small V shapes so the walls look like a knitted sweater.
  10. Gingerbread snow globe: tree, animal and house cookies arranged in a large jar (Taste of Home).

Bigger projects: villages and themed scenes

  1. A village of four or five small houses on one board, each a different colour (Wilton's mini village).
  2. A North Pole cottage with a sign and a sleigh out front.
  3. A princess castle with towers made from ice cream cones.
  4. A pickup truck parked by the house (Wilton has one), with a tiny tree in the back.
  5. A farm: a barn, a fence of pretzels and animal cookies.
  6. A street of graham cracker houses (Taste of Home's Christmas village) behind one gingerbread house.
  7. A train station with the candy train from idea 8.
  8. A house cut from your own home's front: windows and door in the right places.
  9. A "copy the coloring page" house: the design from a page your child coloured (next section).
  10. A cookie scene: stacked tree cookies cut from gingerbread scraps around a small house (Taste of Home).

Plan the design on paper first

Royal icing sets as you work, so a plan helps. Print or draw a simple house front: two walls, a roof line, a door. Let your child colour it in with the candy colours you actually have. Then decorate the house to match.

  1. Lay the candy out and pick four or five colours.
  2. Colour the paper house in those colours only.
  3. Mark where the long items go: candy canes, pretzel rods, wafer rows.
  4. Decorate one wall at a time, copying the page.

For children who like rules, a dot or sticker version of the same outline works too; our dot marker activities page shows how circles can stand for gumdrops. The same paper house drawn in a few lines also appears in our Christmas drawing ideas.

A two-day build plan with drying times

King Arthur Baking's guide gives the times that decide the schedule. Its dough chills for at least an hour before rolling, and the pieces bake at 350°F for 15 to 18 minutes. The walls can take between 4 and 12 hours, depending on the weather and how much icing you used, before the roof goes on. Each roof panel then dries in place for at least an hour before the second one.

When Step
Day 1, morning Mix dough, chill at least 1 hour, roll about 1/4 inch thick, cut and bake
Day 1, afternoon Trim edges with a serrated knife, make royal icing, fit window glass
Day 1, evening Put up the walls on a board with props; leave overnight
Day 2, morning First roof panel, 1 hour, then the second
Day 2, afternoon Decorate

Roof, wall and window details

King Arthur Baking's tips that make the ideas above easier:

  • Trim each panel with a serrated knife, sawing rather than pressing, so the joins meet flat.
  • Pipe a second band of icing along the inside bottom edge of each wall.
  • Use a cereal box to check the corners are square, and an adjustable measuring cup as a prop.
  • For stained-glass windows, melt hard candy pieces on parchment at 250°F for 6 to 8 minutes, cut to size and glue in with icing, before the roof.
  • Leave at least 3/4 inch of dough around each window so the wall stays strong.
  • Keep royal icing covered: it dries quickly and turns lumpy in the air. Meringue powder can replace fresh egg whites.
  • Stand gingerbread people up with a mini marshmallow behind them.

Roof ideas: shredded wheat shingles (Taste of Home), overlapping wafers, cereal rows, sliced almonds as shingles, or plain icing and coarse sugar.

Gingerbread houses in the December calendar

A house takes a weekend, which makes it a good big day in an advent calendar: a card on the Saturday door that says "gingerbread house today". Leftover candy and the paper plan can go on an Elf on the Shelf setup the next morning, and the finished house can sit on the table for Christmas party games.

Questions people ask

What can I use for a gingerbread house roof?

Shredded wheat as shingles, rows of overlapping candy wafers, mini pretzels, cereal, sliced almonds, or plain royal icing with coarse sugar for snow. Taste of Home uses shredded wheat shingles on a cottage and icing with coarse sugar on a ski lodge.

How long does a gingerbread house need to dry before decorating?

King Arthur Baking says the walls can take 4 to 12 hours, depending on the weather and how much icing you used, before the roof goes on. Each roof panel then needs at least an hour.

What is the easiest gingerbread house for kids?

A pre-built kit or a graham cracker house on a plate, with icing in pouches and candy sorted into trays. Wilton uses pre-colored icing pouches on several of its houses and notes that they make cleanup easy. For toddlers, one blogger on the Chinet site hot-glued the pieces together first, which makes the house decoration only.

How do you make stained-glass windows in a gingerbread house?

Cut the window openings before baking. King Arthur Baking melts hard candy pieces on parchment at 250°F for 6 to 8 minutes, then cuts the sheet to size and glues it in with royal icing. Do it before the roof goes on.

Is a gingerbread house edible?

Construction gingerbread is edible; King Arthur Baking says it just has more flour than cookie dough to make it sturdier. A house held together with hot glue is decoration, not food.
