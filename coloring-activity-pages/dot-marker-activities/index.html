What a dot marker page looks like

A dot marker page is a line drawing where the outline, or the whole shape, is made of empty circles. An apple might be twenty circles in a ring with a few for the stalk. The child presses the marker straight down once per circle. Dot stickers work on the same pages, which is handy for a child who isn't ready for paint.

Pages come in a few kinds:

  • Pictures: animals, vehicles, fruit, seasonal shapes.
  • Letters: a capital or lowercase letter made of circles, often with a picture that starts with that sound.
  • Numbers: the numeral in circles, plus that many objects to dot.
  • Shapes: a page of mixed shapes where the child dots only the circles, or only the triangles.
  • Names: the child's own name in dot letters.

Dot marker activities for toddlers

Read the label on your set before anything else. The Do-A-Dot Art sets listed by Target and by Becker's School Supplies both say "3 years and up" and carry the warning "Choking hazard, small parts. Not for children under 3." The CPSC explains that products intended for children under 3 may not contain small parts; products for older children with small parts have to carry this kind of label.

So for a child under 3, choose a set labelled for their age if you can find one, or use large round stickers instead. Whatever you use, sit with them, and keep caps away: the American Academy of Pediatrics lists pen and marker caps among household choking hazards.

Good first pages for the youngest:

  • One big shape per page: a ball, a sun, a balloon.
  • Six to ten large circles, well apart.
  • A single colour, so the job is "fill them all" with no rules about which colour goes where.

Dot marker activities for 3 year olds

CDC's three-year milestone list includes drawing a circle when you show how. By four it includes naming a few colours and holding a crayon between fingers and thumb. Pages that fit that stretch:

  • Colour-coded pictures: red circles for the apple, green for the leaf.
  • Shape pages: "dot only the circles."
  • Patterns: red, blue, red, blue along a caterpillar.
  • Counting pages: the numeral 3 in circles and three ducks to dot.
  • First letters: the first letter of the child's name, then the whole name.

Letter, number and sight word pages

Dot pages are a gentle way into letters and numbers because the child doesn't have to form the shape yet, just follow it. Ideas:

  • Alphabet pages, one letter a day or a letter of the week.
  • Upper and lowercase pairs side by side.
  • Number pages from 1 to 10, then ten-frames: a 2 × 5 grid of circles to fill up to the number.
  • Sight words for early readers: each letter of "the" or "and" in circles.
  • Name sheets: the child's name in dot letters, which you can make yourself in a word processor with a dotted font, or by drawing circles round printed letters.

Activities that need no printer

  • Connect the dots: the child stamps dots anywhere, then joins them with a pencil.
  • Dot tic-tac-toe: draw a grid, two players, two colours.
  • Patterns: start a row of dots in two colours and let the child continue it.
  • Tape resist: stick painter's tape across a sheet, dot all over, then peel the tape.
  • Negative space: tape a paper shape onto a sheet, dot round it, lift the shape off.
  • Dot faces and creatures: turn each dot into a face, a bug or a monster with a pen.

These come from The Inspired Treehouse's and Speech Blubs' activity lists, which have more.

Printing dot marker pages so they work

  1. Measure your marker. Stamp one dot on scrap paper and measure it with a ruler.
  2. Check the page. The circles should be at least as wide as that dot; if they are smaller, every dot spills over its circle and the picture is harder to read.
  3. Print at actual size. Choose "100%" or "actual size" in the print dialog, not "fit to page", which can shrink the circles.
  4. Use heavier paper. Card stock takes wet paint better than thin copy paper.
  5. Laminate a copy for reuse with dot stickers or dry-erase pens, and keep paper copies for paint. Laminated pages also fit a busy book binder; see busy book ideas for pages to go with them.

Labels worth reading on the set

Look for the ACMI AP (Approved Product) seal. ACMI says it marks art materials that a toxicologist has found to contain nothing in sufficient quantities to be toxic or injurious to humans, including children, and that it is meant for children in sixth grade or younger. ACMI also says never to give a child in sixth grade or younger a product with its CL (Cautionary Labeling) seal.

"Washable" on the box is the maker's claim about clothes and skin; follow the washing instructions on that set. Our how to use dot markers guide has more on labels, storage and clean-up. For a party table, dot pages also make a calm station between party games for kids.

Questions people ask

What are dot marker activities?

Pages and games for dot markers, the bottle markers with a round sponge tip that stamp a dot when pressed. The classic is a printable page of circles that form a picture, letter or number; the child fills each circle with a dot.

Are dot markers OK for toddlers?

Check the set. Do-A-Dot Art sets sold at Target and Becker's are labelled 3 years and up with a small-parts choking warning for under-3s. If you use any art material with a younger child, stay with them, and keep caps out of reach: the AAP lists pen and marker caps as choking hazards.

What can I use instead of dot markers?

Round stickers, a cotton bud dipped in paint, or crayons to colour the circles. Dot stickers work on the same printable pages.

What paper is best for dot marker pages?

Dot markers lay down wet paint, so thin paper can buckle or soak through. Card stock or heavier paper holds up better; slip a tray or old placemat underneath.

Are dot marker printables free?

Many are. When you download one, check the circle size against your marker and print at actual size, not 'fit to page', so the circles don't shrink.
