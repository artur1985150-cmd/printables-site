In short

  • Split into two teams; one player draws a Christmas word in silence while their team guesses before the timer runs out.
  • The official game gives the drawer one minute; for children and long song titles, 90 seconds keeps it fair.
  • No letters, numbers, talking or sign language. Syllables and sound-alike pictures are allowed, so agree on that before you start.
  • Easy words (tree, star, mitten) suit young children; phrases like 'Santa stuck in the chimney' suit adults.
  • A whiteboard is easiest for a crowd; paper on a clipboard or a phone drawing app works for a small table.

Christmas Pictionary is the drawing game played with holiday words: one player sketches a word like "snowman" or "Jingle Bells" without speaking, and their team guesses before the timer runs out. Below are our word lists from easy to hard, with drawing hints for the tricky ones, plus the house rules that stop arguments halfway through the game.

How to play Christmas Pictionary

Pictionary was invented by Robert Angel and first published in 1985, according to Wikipedia. The Christmas version needs no board, just words, a timer and something to draw on.

  1. Split into two teams. Mix ages so every team has someone who can draw and someone who guesses fast.
  2. Write the words on slips, fold them and put them in a bowl. Or print a list and cut it up.
  3. The first drawer picks a slip and looks at it without showing anyone.
  4. Start the timer. The drawer sketches; their team shouts guesses.
  5. A correct guess before time runs out scores one point. Then the other team sends up a drawer.
  6. Change the drawer every turn, so everyone gets a go.

Mattel's rule sheet for the classic board game gives the drawer one minute on a sand timer. The pages we read pick different times: 101 Planners suggests 90 seconds for children, songs and long phrases, and Fun Party Pop plays three-minute turns where the drawer keeps taking new words until the buzzer. Happy Mom Hacks adds a steal: if the drawing team misses, the other team gets one guess for the point.

Play until every player has drawn at least once, or until one team reaches 10 points.

What the drawer can and cannot do

Most fights in Pictionary start with a drawer who writes a letter "just to help". The official instruction sheet settles it.

The drawer can:

  • draw anything connected to the word, however loose the link;
  • split a word into syllables and draw each part;
  • draw a word that sounds the same, such as "dock" for "doc".

The drawer cannot:

  • write letters or numbers;
  • draw dashes for the number of letters, or the ear sign for "sounds like";
  • speak to the team;
  • use sign language.

The sheet also says that teams should agree at the start how exact an answer has to be. Is "bunk" good enough for "bunk bed"? Decide before the first turn. A simple house rule for Christmas words: the guess must contain every main word on the slip, so "gingerbread" alone does not count for "gingerbread house", but "a gingerbread house" does.

Christmas songs and films to draw

Titles are the hardest round, so give them the longer timer. The drawer can break a title into its words and draw each one, as the official rules allow syllables.

Before you start the titles round, tell everyone it is a titles round. Guessers then shout film names instead of single objects, which speeds things up.

The "same word at once" race comes from the board game's All Play squares, where every team's drawer sketches the same word and the first team to guess wins the round. It keeps a big room busy, because nobody waits for the other team.

For the word slips, plain printer paper is fine for one night. If the cards will come out again next year, card stock lasts longer. You can also use the same slips for a round of charades when the drawers get tired; the charades rules are similar, only with acting instead of drawing.

Make it work for children and mixed ages

Children and grandparents can play on the same team if the bowl holds two sets of slips. Fold the easy words one way and the hard ones another, and let each drawer pick from the right pile. Give the youngest 90 seconds and let them point at their picture.

Keep score loosely when small children are playing. A drawing that makes the room laugh can get a bonus point, even if nobody guessed it.

Questions people ask

How do you play Christmas Pictionary?

Split into two teams and put folded word slips in a bowl. A player from one team picks a slip, then draws the word without speaking, writing letters or using numbers. Their team calls out guesses. A correct guess before the timer runs out scores a point, then the other team takes a turn with a new drawer.

How long should each Pictionary turn be?

The official Mattel game uses a one-minute sand timer. For young children, long phrases or song titles, give 90 seconds. Some families play three-minute turns where the drawer keeps going through new words until the time is up.

What are good Christmas Pictionary words for kids?

Short things with a clear shape: tree, star, bell, candy cane, snowman, stocking, present, mitten, wreath, candle, gingerbread man and reindeer. Young children do better with these than with films, songs or actions.

Can you draw letters or numbers in Pictionary?

No. The official rules ban letters and numbers, dashes that show how many letters a word has, and the ear sign for 'sounds like'. You may break a word into syllables and draw a word that sounds the same, such as 'dock' for 'doc'.

What do you need to play Christmas Pictionary?

A word list or cards cut into slips, a bowl, a timer, and something to draw on: a whiteboard or easel pad for a big group, or paper and markers for a small table. Keep a pen and a scrap of paper for the score.
