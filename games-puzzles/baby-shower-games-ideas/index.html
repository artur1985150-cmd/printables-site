In short

  • Ask the parent-to-be first: the Emily Post Institute says games are optional and should be ones the honoree wants.
  • For a large group, use paper games everyone plays at once in their seat: bingo, word scramble, nursery rhyme fill-ins, price guessing.
  • For a small group, games where people talk work better: advice cards, who knows mom best, guess the baby photo.
  • Two or three games is plenty; gift opening alone can take 30 to 60 minutes for 30 guests, per the Emily Post Institute.
  • Print one sheet and one pen per guest, plus an answer key for the host, and have small prizes ready.

The best baby shower games ideas match the room. With a big group, pick paper games everyone plays at once from their seat, like baby bingo, a word scramble or a nursery rhyme fill-in. With a small group, pick games that get people talking, like advice cards or guess the baby photo. Two or three games is enough, and the parent-to-be gets the final say.

That last point comes from the Emily Post Institute: games are optional, and the host should check with the honoree which ones they'd enjoy.

Before you pick: ask the parent-to-be

The Emily Post Institute says showers don't have to include games, and that many honorees and guests don't care for them. Its advice to hosts:

  • Ask the honoree whether they want games, and which.
  • If the shower is a surprise, check with them when they arrive before announcing the games.
  • Let anyone who wants to sit a game out do so; they can still watch and cheer.

Ask about the games involving the honoree's body in particular. The Institute says the belly-measuring game must be run past the mom or surrogate first, and Happiest Baby calls it one of the older games that newer ones have replaced.

Best baby shower games for a large group

The Institute recommends keeping a shower to around 20 to 30 guests at most. At that size, the games that work are the ones nobody has to stand up for. Everyone gets a sheet and a pen and plays at the same time.

Best baby shower games for a small group

With eight or ten people, talking games are better than quiet sheets.

  • Who knows mom best. Guests answer questions about the mom-to-be (her first job, her favourite food); she reads out the right answers.
  • Guess the baby photo. Guests bring a baby photo of themselves; pin them up and match faces to names.
  • Advice for the parents. The Institute lists this one: each guest fills in a card with their best advice, and the host awards prizes for the best or funniest.
  • Guess the birth stats. Weight, length, hair colour, date. The Institute notes the winner can only be chosen after the birth, so keep the cards.
  • Building baby's library. Another from the Institute: pass a notebook round and have each guest write their favourite children's book and why.
  • New baby mad libs. The host writes a short story about the first months with the baby, blanks out words, and guests fill them in before the stories are read aloud.

Baby shower games for mixed or co-ed groups

When partners, friends and family who don't know each other are all there, pick games that need no prior knowledge and break the ice:

  • Guess the baby photo, which gets strangers talking at once.
  • Baby food taste test. The Institute's version: buy 6 to 10 jars of baby food depending on numbers, cover the labels, number the jars and let guests guess. It recommends this over the melted-chocolate-in-a-diaper version, which can put guests off. Check the labels if anyone has a food allergy.
  • Emoji pictionary or baby charades, played in two teams.
  • Price guessing, where the people who've bought baby gear before have an edge.

Printable baby shower games: what to prepare

Paper games are cheap and easy to run. A prep list for the host:

  1. Print one sheet per guest for each paper game, plus five spares.
  2. Print or write an answer key for each game and keep it out of sight.
  3. Put a pen on every seat, or a cup of pens on each table.
  4. Have a timer on your phone for timed games.
  5. Wrap the prizes. The Institute suggests hand lotion or soaps, chocolates, kitchen towels or a small candle holder with a candle.
  6. Decide the order: an ice-breaker as people arrive, bingo during gift opening, one more game with dessert.
  7. Keep the advice cards and guess sheets in an envelope for the parents.

Print on plain US letter or A4 paper; a game sheet doesn't need card stock. If children are coming, set up a separate corner with ideas from our party games for kids. Many of these games also work at other parties: see our bridal shower games, Thanksgiving games and Christmas party games.

How many games to plan

Two or three. Think about the whole afternoon: arrivals, food, gift opening, cake and goodbyes. One ice-breaker, bingo during the gifts and one more game if the room wants it is plenty. Stop while people are still enjoying it; the Institute says a host shouldn't push guests past that point.

After the shower

Whoever records the gifts during bingo gives the parent-to-be the list the thank-you cards are written from. The Emily Post Institute recommends asking one guest to do this as the gifts are opened. Questions people ask

What are the best baby shower games to play?

The ones the parent-to-be likes. Safe crowd-pleasers are baby bingo played during gift opening, a baby word scramble, a nursery rhyme fill-in, guess the baby photo and advice cards for the parents.

How many games should you play at a baby shower?

Two or three is usually enough. Leave time for food and gift opening, which the Emily Post Institute estimates at 30 to 60 minutes for 30 guests.

What are good baby shower games for a large group?

Paper games that everyone plays at once without moving: baby bingo, word scrambles, nursery rhyme fill-ins, a price-guessing sheet and a pass-the-pen advice card. They need one printout and one pen per guest.

What are good baby shower games for a small group?

Games built on conversation: who knows mom best, guess the baby photo, advice cards read aloud, and guess the birth stats. With fewer people, everyone gets a turn.

What prizes do you give for baby shower games?

Small and useful. The Emily Post Institute suggests things like hand lotions or soaps, chocolates, kitchen towels or a candle.
