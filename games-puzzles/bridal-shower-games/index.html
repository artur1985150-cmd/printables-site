Before you plan: ask the bride

The Emily Post Institute's advice on shower games, written for baby showers, is to check with the guest of honor that games would be welcome, and it says a good host doesn't push guests into structured activities "past the point of enjoyment." For a surprise shower, it suggests checking the plan with the honoree once she arrives, before announcing any games.

Things to ask her:

  • Does she want games at all, or one activity like advice cards?
  • Which games does she dislike?
  • Will older relatives or colleagues be there, and does that change anything?
  • Is anything off limits: lingerie jokes, questions about the couple's past?

The Emily Post Institute puts the ideal shower two months to two weeks before the wedding, so there is usually time to collect answers for the quiz games.

How many bridal shower games to play

Paperless Post suggests two to four games, or one long or two short games per hour. Modern MOH suggests three to five games or activities, roughly one per hour. In practice, plan one game to start the party, one during gift opening, and one or two others, then drop any that the room doesn't need.

Classic bridal shower games and how to play them

Bridal bingo. Each guest gets a card with gifts the bride might receive (towels, a stand mixer, a frame) instead of numbers. As she opens presents, guests mark what they hear. First full line calls "bingo". It keeps guests busy during the longest part of the shower.

He said, she said. Before the shower, collect answers or quotes from both partners ("who said I love you first?"). Read them aloud or print them; guests mark "he" or "she". The bride reveals the answers. In a same-sex couple, use the two names.

How well do you know the bride. Ask the bride a list of questions a week ahead: first car, favorite takeout, where the couple met. Guests answer on a sheet; the bride reads her answers and guests score themselves.

Would she rather. Similar, but with pairs of choices (beach or mountains, cake or pie). The bride's answers are collected first; the most correct guesses wins.

Toilet paper wedding dress. Split guests into teams, give each a few rolls of toilet paper and some tape, and each team dresses one member as a bride. Paperless Post's paper version has a 15-minute limit; Modern MOH suggests five to ten minutes. The bride picks the winner.

Advice cards. Each guest fills in a card with advice for the marriage, or a date-night idea. They are read aloud or kept for the couple. For its advice-card game, the Emily Post Institute says winners can be chosen at random from everyone who filled in a card.

The ring or clothespin game. Each guest gets a clothespin or plastic ring on arrival. Choose a banned word, such as "wedding" or "bride". Anyone who hears another guest say it takes their pin. Whoever has the most at the end wins.

Find the guest. A card with lines like "has been to Italy" or "met the bride at school"; guests find a person for each line and collect names. It works well when the guests don't know each other.

Musical bouquet. Guests pass a bouquet while music plays. When it stops, whoever holds it answers a question or gives advice, then the music starts again.

The shoe game. For a couples shower: the couple sit back to back, each holding one of their own shoes and one of the partner's. A host reads questions ("who is the better cook?") and they raise the shoe of whoever the answer is.

Bridal shower games for large groups

With a big guest list, choose games everyone plays at once from their seat. Bridal Shower 101 says charades is good for larger groups, and Modern MOH has a separate list of games for larger groups.

Funny bridal shower games

If the bride and guests like a laugh:

  • Toilet paper wedding dress.
  • Rom-com charades, with film titles on slips.
  • How old was the bride: guess her age in childhood photos.
  • Bridal mad libs: guests fill in words for a story about the couple.
  • What's in your purse: points for items on a list, such as a lipstick or a receipt.
  • Emoji pictionary: translate rows of emoji into wedding words.

Read the room before the lingerie-style games. Modern MOH notes that some games only suit groups who are comfortable with them.

Printable bridal shower games and prep

Most of the classics are paper games. To print:

  1. Bingo cards, one per guest.
  2. He said she said and quiz sheets, plus an answer key for the host.
  3. Advice cards.
  4. Find the guest cards.
  5. Word scramble or word search.
  6. Charades slips.

Collect the couple's answers at least a week ahead, print on card stock, put a pen at every seat, and keep each game in its own envelope with the answer key. Have a few small prizes ready; the Emily Post Institute suggests giving them to winners who played "with good humor and grace."

Questions people ask

How many games should you play at a bridal shower?

Two to five. Paperless Post suggests two to four games, or one long or two short games per hour, and Modern MOH suggests three to five, roughly one per hour. Leave time for food, gifts and talking, and drop a game if the room is enjoying itself without it.

What are easy bridal shower games?

Bridal bingo during gift opening, advice cards, the ring or clothespin game, would she rather and a wedding word scramble. They need a printout or a small prop, take a minute to explain, and guests can play them from their seat.

What bridal shower games work for large groups?

Paper games everyone plays at once: bridal bingo, he said she said, how well do you know the bride, a word search or scramble, and find the guest. For team games like the toilet paper dress, split the room into teams of four or five.

How do you play he said she said at a bridal shower?

Before the shower, collect quotes or answers from both partners. Read each one aloud or print them on a sheet, and guests mark whether the bride or the groom said it. The bride reveals the answers and the guest with the most right wins.

Do you have to have games at a bridal shower?

No. The Emily Post Institute says a good host checks with the honoree that games would be welcome and doesn't push guests past the point of enjoyment. Some showers swap games for a single activity, such as advice cards or a recipe collection.
