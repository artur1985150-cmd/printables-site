A Christmas scavenger hunt is either a clue trail, where each clue sends players to the next hiding spot until they reach a prize, or a list of Christmas things to find in any order. Below are 20 rhyming clues of our own with answers, an indoor and an outdoor list, and a step-by-step setup that works at home, in a classroom or at a party.

Clue trail or item list: which hunt to run

Both are scavenger hunts, but they play differently.

A clue trail is a chain. Clue 1 is handed over, its answer is where clue 2 waits, and so on to the prize. One group follows it together, which makes it good for a family on Christmas morning. Funattic suggests 8 to 12 clues for a classic trail.

An item list is a race. Everyone gets the same list and looks at once; the first to find everything wins. It suits parties with more children. Paperless Post suggests splitting the group into two or three teams so older children can help younger ones, and having a goodie bag ready for the first team to finish.

Christmas scavenger hunt clues for kids

Twelve easy clues we wrote, each pointing to a spot most homes have. Swap or skip any that don't fit your house, and change the order so each answer is where the next card hides.

  1. I keep the milk and butter cold. Open my door, your next clue's told. (Fridge)
  2. Wash your hands and brush your teeth. Look at the mat that lies beneath. (Bathroom mat)
  3. Socks go on first, then I go after. Look inside me, and hear the laughter! (Shoes or boots)
  4. I'm green and tall with lights all around. Look under my branches, down near the ground. (Christmas tree)
  5. Hung up and waiting on Christmas Eve, look in the toe before you leave. (Stocking)
  6. You sleep on me every night. Look under the pillow, hold on tight. (Bed)
  7. I hold your cereal, your soup and stew. Open my cupboard to find a clue. (Bowl cupboard)
  8. Knock knock, ring ring, who could it be? Look by the place where you turn the key. (Front door)
  9. Cartoons and films are on my screen. Look by the remote, and see what's been seen. (Television)
  10. Bubbles and ducks and a splashy floor. Look in me, then look for more. (Bath)
  11. Round and round to get clothes clean. Peek inside the washing machine. (Washing machine; check it is empty and switched off first)
  12. Wrapped in paper, tied with a bow. Your prize is here, ho ho ho! (Where the prize is hidden)

Skip the oven, the fireplace and anything high on a shelf for younger players. An oven mitt drawer or a cookie tin is a safer kitchen spot.

Harder clues for teens and adults

These take more thinking. Play Party Plan suggests letting teens and adults use their phones for its song-based clues; ours need no phone.

  1. I have keys but open no doors. Play me a carol and you'll find more. (Piano or keyboard; a laptop works too)
  2. A face and two hands, but no arms at all. I count down to Christmas from up on the wall. (Wall clock)
  3. Four legs, no feet, and a feast on my top on the 25th. Look underneath. (Dining table)
  4. Every morning I'm full of beans, yet I'm no plate of breakfast. (Coffee maker or coffee jar)
  5. Everyone sits here, nobody stays long. Check the spare roll. (Bathroom)
  6. Pages and pages and I never speak. Find the ghost of Christmas past on my shelf. (A copy of A Christmas Carol, or any book on the bookshelf)
  7. I carry you everywhere but never leave the driveway alone. (The car; an adult opens it first)
  8. Jack Frost would move in if he could: I'm colder than the fridge. (Freezer)

For a party crowd that likes this kind of puzzle, add a round from our Christmas trivia questions at the prize spot: the team has to answer three before they open it.

Christmas scavenger hunt lists

Item lists need no chain, so they are faster to set up. Hide the items you need to, or use things already out for the season.

Indoor list:

  • A candy cane
  • Something red and something green
  • A bell
  • A pine cone or a sprig of greenery
  • A star
  • Something that sparkles
  • A ribbon or a bow
  • A gift tag
  • Something that smells of cinnamon or ginger
  • A snowman (a toy, a picture, a decoration)
  • A Christmas card
  • A sock that isn't a stocking

Outdoor or lights-hunt list:

  • A house with blue lights
  • An inflatable in a front garden
  • A wreath on a door
  • A star on a roof or in a window
  • A reindeer decoration
  • A snowman, real or not
  • Candy cane lights along a path
  • A tree lit up in a window
  • A red bow on a gate, letterbox or car
  • A nativity scene

For an outdoor hunt, an adult walks with each group, players stay on the pavement, and anyone out after dark wears something light-coloured. Funattic suggests photos instead of collecting items on its winter nature hunt; for a lights hunt, a tick on the sheet does the same job and leaves the neighbours' gardens alone.

How to set up a Christmas scavenger hunt

  1. Count your players and pick a format from the table above.
  2. Choose 8 to 12 spots for a trail, fewer for small children.
  3. Write the clues, then number them. Play Party Plan numbers its cards and keeps a cheat sheet; do the same so you can rescue a stuck team.
  4. Print on card stock if the clues will be handled a lot, as Play Party Plan suggests.
  5. Hide in reverse: the prize first, then the clue that leads to it, and so on back to clue 2.
  6. Hide clue 1 somewhere fun. Play Party Plan's ideas include inside a wrapped gift or a toilet paper roll.
  7. Walk the whole route yourself, cheat sheet in hand.
  8. Tell players the house rules before they start: which rooms are out of bounds, no climbing, put things back.

Running two teams at once? Give each team its own colour of card and a different order of the same spots, so they don't simply follow each other.

Making it work for every age and ability

A hunt only works if everyone in the group can reach the clues.

  • Non-readers: draw the answer or print a picture on the card (a fridge, a tree), or let an older child read aloud.
  • Toddlers: five or six clues, one or two rooms, everything at their eye level.
  • Mixed ages: pair a reader with a non-reader, and make finding the card the little one's job.
  • A player who uses a wheelchair or can't do stairs: keep every spot on one floor and below shoulder height, and leave clear routes between rooms.
  • Players who don't speak much English: picture clues work in any language.
  • Competitive siblings: run it as one family team with one shared prize, such as the puzzle gift Play Party Plan ends on.

Questions people ask

How do you set up a Christmas scavenger hunt?

Pick 8 to 12 hiding spots, write a clue for each that points to the next, number the cards, and keep a cheat sheet of answers. Hide them in reverse order, starting at the prize, then hand over clue 1. Walk the route once to check every card is where it should be.

What are some good Christmas scavenger hunt clues?

Clues that rhyme and point to an everyday spot: 'I keep the milk and butter cold, open my door, your next clue's told' (the fridge) or 'I'm green and tall with lights all around, look under my branches, down near the ground' (the Christmas tree).

What can you put at the end of a Christmas scavenger hunt?

A wrapped gift for the whole family, a box of sweets to share, a new board game, or Christmas pyjamas. Play Party Plan ends its hunt on a puzzle gift that the finders complete to see what the present is.

How do you do a Christmas scavenger hunt for toddlers?

Use five or six picture clues at a toddler's eye level in one or two rooms, and let an adult read or point. A list hunt with pictures (find something red, find a bell) works better than riddles at that age.
