In short

  • Each player gets a different card; the caller draws words at random and players mark them; the first full line wins.
  • Use 3x3 or 4x4 cards for small children and 5x5 for older players and adults.
  • The call list needs many more words than the card has squares, or every card ends up holding the same words.
  • Below is a 60-word Christmas call list you can copy, cut into slips and draw from a hat.
  • Decide before the first round what happens when two players shout bingo on the same call.

Christmas bingo is ordinary bingo with holiday words or pictures in place of numbers. Every player gets a different card, a caller draws words one by one, players cover the matching squares, and the first to complete a row, column or diagonal shouts "bingo". It works for a table of four or a room of forty, because each person only needs a card and some markers.

How to play Christmas bingo

  1. Give each player one card and a handful of markers.
  2. The caller mixes the call slips in a hat or bowl.
  3. The caller draws one slip, reads the word aloud and puts the slip aside. Do not put it back.
  4. Players with that word on their card cover it.
  5. The first player with a full line shouts "bingo".
  6. The caller checks the winner's covered squares against the slips already drawn. If they match, that player wins the round. If not, play goes on.

Monday Mandala asks the caller to keep a slow pace so everyone finds their squares. With young children, show the picture as well as saying the word. 101 Planners suggests checking the card against the calls before declaring a winner, which is why the drawn slips go on a separate pile.

Card sizes: 3x3, 4x4 and 5x5

Both printable pages we read offer three grid sizes. The bigger the grid, the longer each round.

3x3 9 Optional, in the middle 3 in a row Toddlers and preschoolers
4x4 16 None (an even grid has no middle square) 4 in a row Early readers
5x5 25 Middle square 5 in a row Older children and adults

The 5x5 layout comes from number bingo. Wikipedia describes the standard American card as five columns and five rows under the letters B-I-N-G-O, with a free space in the centre and the numbers 1 to 75 split across the columns. A Christmas card keeps the grid and the free middle square and swaps the numbers for words.

How many words the call list needs

This is the part printable pages tend to skip, and it decides whether the game works.

If your call list has exactly as many words as the card has squares, every card holds the same words in a different order. In a blackout round (cover the whole card) everyone finishes on the same call. Even in a line round, too many people win at once.

So the list has to be longer than the card. Our rule of thumb is about twice the squares:

  • 3x3 cards: around 18 words
  • 4x4 cards: around 32 words
  • 5x5 cards with a free middle: around 48 to 60 words

A longer list means longer rounds, since more of the calls hit nobody's card. A shorter list means faster rounds and more ties. For a group of very young children, a short list and a 3x3 card is the right trade.

Winning patterns for more rounds

A straight line is the default. Wikipedia names a few other patterns from number bingo that carry over well:

  • Four corners: cover only the four corner squares. Quick, good for little ones.
  • Postage stamp: a 2x2 block in any corner.
  • Blackout, also called coverall: every square on the card. Wikipedia notes church games often end on a coverall.

101 Planners adds Christmas shapes such as a tree, a candy cane, a present and a wreath, drawn on a card of the same grid. Show the shape on a sample card before the round so nobody argues afterwards.

A good evening runs two or three line rounds, one pattern round and a blackout to finish.

Two winners on the same call

Neither of the printable pages says what to do here, so settle it before you start. Three fair options:

  • Both win and both get a prize. The simplest, if you bought spares.
  • Draw one more slip. The first of the two players to have that word on their card wins; if neither does, draw again.
  • The tied players say a Christmas word in turn until one repeats or hesitates.

Write your choice on the call sheet so the caller doesn't have to decide on the spot.

Christmas bingo for big groups, classrooms and adults

For a group of more than 30, Monday Mandala suggests pairing guests or playing in teams, so you print fewer cards. Half-page cards (two per sheet) save paper too.

In a classroom, 101 Planners recommends four cards per page and short rounds. For adults, try music bingo: the caller plays a few seconds of a carol and players cover the song title. Film bingo works the same way with stills or quotes.

Card stock lasts longer than copy paper if cards are passed around, and laminated cards can be used again with a dry-erase marker, as both printable pages suggest.

Questions people ask

How do you play Christmas bingo?

Give each player a different card with Christmas words or pictures. The caller draws one word at a time from a hat and reads it out, and players cover matching squares. The first player with a full row, column or diagonal shouts bingo; the caller checks the card against the drawn slips.

How many words do you need for Christmas bingo?

More than the card has squares, or every card carries the same words. We suggest about twice the squares: 18 words for 3x3 cards, 32 for 4x4 and around 48 to 60 for 5x5 cards with a free middle square.

What words go on a Christmas bingo card?

Things everyone recognises: Santa, reindeer, sleigh, snowman, stocking, candy cane, gingerbread man, wreath, mistletoe, ornament, star, bell, present, elf, chimney, cookies, hot chocolate, mittens, snowflake, and so on. Our 60-word list below is ready to use.

What can you use as bingo markers?

Anything small and flat: buttons, coins, paper circles, or sweets such as chocolate buttons. 101 Planners also suggests candy, marshmallows or dry-erase markers on laminated cards.

What prizes are good for Christmas bingo?

Small, cheap and wrapped: a chocolate bar, a candy cane, a mug, hand warmers, a festive pair of socks, or a printable coupon such as 'skip the washing-up'. Plan one prize per round plus a slightly bigger one for the final blackout.
