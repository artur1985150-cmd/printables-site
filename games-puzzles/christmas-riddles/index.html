In short

  • 40 Christmas riddles with answers: easy ones for children, puns for the middle round, and word tricks for adults.
  • Easy riddles describe a thing; medium ones hang on a pun; hard ones need you to rearrange letters or think sideways.
  • Play in teams: read a riddle, allow 30 seconds, give one hint, and score 2 points without the hint and 1 with it.
  • A riddle either describes something in a roundabout way or rests on a pun; both are easy to write yourself.
  • Riddles fit Christmas crackers, advent calendar doors and treasure hunt clues as well as a quiz round.

Christmas riddles are short puzzles about holiday things, answered in a word or two: "I hang by the fireplace empty all year and wake up full one night. What am I?" (A stocking.) Below are 40 riddles with answers, grouped from easy to hard, then a way to play them as a team game and a method for writing your own.

What makes a riddle easy or hard

Wikipedia splits riddles into two kinds. An enigma describes something in roundabout, metaphorical language and needs careful thinking. A conundrum depends on a pun in the question or the answer.

We sorted the riddles on this page by what the solver has to do:

Level What you have to do Good for
Easy Picture the thing from a description Young children
Medium Spot the pun Older children, mixed groups
Carol and film titles Match a clue to a title Anyone who knows the songs
Hard Rearrange letters or think sideways Teenagers and adults

Easy Christmas riddles for kids

  1. I'm green all year, but only in December do people dress me up in lights. What am I? A Christmas tree.
  2. I hang by the fireplace empty all year, and one night I wake up full. What am I? A stocking.
  3. I have a carrot for a nose and coal for eyes, and the sun is my worst enemy. What am I? A snowman.
  4. I'm striped red and white and shaped like a walking stick, but you'd never lean on me. What am I? A candy cane.
  5. Two people pull me, I go bang, and I give you a paper hat. What am I? A Christmas cracker.
  6. I sit at the very top of the tree and watch the presents pile up below. What am I? The star (or the angel).
  7. I wear bright paper and a bow, and everyone wants to rip my clothes off on Christmas morning. What am I? A present.
  8. I fall without a sound, I'm white, and I melt in your warm hand. What am I? A snowflake.
  9. You can live in me if you're very small, and you can eat my roof. What am I? A gingerbread house.
  10. I'm not a phone, but I ring on every sleigh. What am I? A bell.
  11. I have antlers, I can fly one night a year, and my nose is famous. Who am I? Rudolph.
  12. I come down the chimney and never ring the doorbell. Who am I? Santa.

The cracker in riddle 5 is a fair answer for children in Britain and Ireland. Wikipedia lists crackers as part of Christmas in the United Kingdom, Ireland and Commonwealth countries such as Australia, Canada and New Zealand; elsewhere children may not know them.

Medium riddles: Christmas puns

  1. Why did the Christmas tree get a job at the bank? It was good with branches.
  2. Why don't stockings ever argue? They just hang out.
  3. Why did the candle leave the advent wreath? It felt burned out.
  4. How does a fir tree check its messages? It logs on.
  5. Why did the snowman turn down a hot drink? He didn't want to lose his head.
  6. Why was the advent calendar so tired? Its days were numbered.
  7. Why was the gift tag so confident? It knew exactly who it was for.
  8. What did the bauble say when it fell off the tree? "I'm having a smashing time."
  9. Why did the elf sit on the clock? He wanted to be on Santa time.
  10. Why was the wreath always invited back? It went round so well.

Puns land better read aloud than on paper. Pause before the answer.

Carol and film title riddles

Each answer is the name of a carol, song or film. These work well between trivia rounds.

  1. Which carol would a town with no noise sing? Silent Night.
  2. Which carol is an order to decorate? Deck the Halls.
  3. Which song is a weather wish repeated three times? Let It Snow! Let It Snow! Let It Snow!
  4. Which song is a snowman's name tag? Frosty the Snowman.
  5. Which film would a burglar regret? Home Alone.
  6. Which film is a train timetable for the North Pole? The Polar Express.
  7. Which film is about a very tall helper who thinks he's an elf? Elf.
  8. Which song is a counting exercise with a partridge at the start? The Twelve Days of Christmas.

Hard Christmas riddles for adults

  1. Which Christmas word turns into a man's name when you read it backwards? Noel (Leon).
  2. Rearrange my five letters and you'll find me on a reef. Leave them alone and you can sing me. What am I? A carol (coral).
  3. Take away my first letter and I become this very moment. What am I? Snow (now).
  4. The more of me you take off on Christmas morning, the bigger the pile on the floor. What am I? Wrapping paper.
  5. I'm written once a year by small hands and sent to an address with no street. What am I? A letter to Santa.
  6. I spend January leaving little green pins in your carpet. What am I? A real Christmas tree.
  7. I get shorter the longer I work, and I cry while I do it. What am I? A candle.
  8. Twelve of us arrive, one more each day, and by the end there are 364 of us. What are we? The gifts in The Twelve Days of Christmas.
  9. I'm hung up on purpose, people meet under me on purpose, and I'm only welcome in December. What am I? Mistletoe.
  10. I have lots of little doors, but you're only allowed to open one a day. What am I? An advent calendar.

A note on riddle 38: the true love gives one gift on day one, three on day two (two new plus the repeat), six on day three, and so on. Add the twelve days together and the total is 364. Have a calculator ready, because someone will check.

How to run a Christmas riddle game

The riddle pages we read give lists but no rules, so here is a format that works at a table of eight or a room of thirty.

  1. Split into teams of three to five and give each team paper and a pen.
  2. Pick ten riddles: three easy, three medium, two titles, two hard.
  3. Read a riddle twice. Teams have 30 seconds to write an answer.
  4. If no team has it, give one hint (for riddle 33, "it's white and cold") and 30 more seconds.
  5. Score 2 points for an answer without the hint and 1 with it.
  6. Reveal the answer after each riddle, not at the end, so people groan while they still remember the question.

For a tie, read one more hard riddle; the first team to hold up the right answer wins.

How to write your own Christmas riddle

Both kinds Wikipedia describes are easy to make at home.

For a description riddle (an enigma):

  1. Pick the answer: a mince pie, the fairy lights, grandad's armchair.
  2. List what it does, looks like or how people treat it, without naming it.
  3. Pick the two or three oddest facts and write them as "I" sentences.
  4. End with "What am I?" and test it on one person.

For a pun riddle (a conundrum), start from a word with two meanings: branches (tree or bank), log (wood or sign in), wrap (paper or finish). Build a question where only the second meaning makes sense.

Family riddles about your own house beat any list. "I'm the chair nobody sits in until Grandma arrives" only works in one living room, which is the point.

Where to use Christmas riddles

  • Christmas crackers. Wikipedia says the slip inside a cracker carries a motto, a joke, a riddle or a piece of trivia. Home-made crackers can hold your own.
  • Advent calendars: one riddle behind each door, with the answer the next day. Our advent calendar ideas have more fillers.
  • Treasure hunt clues: each answer is where the next clue is hidden. "I keep the milk cold" sends children to the fridge.
  • Place cards at dinner, with the answer under the plate.
  • A warm-up while guests arrive, before Christmas charades or a round of Christmas bingo.

Questions people ask

What is a good Christmas riddle for kids?

Try: 'I hang by the fireplace empty all year, and one night I wake up full. What am I?' A stocking. Or: 'I have a carrot for a nose and the sun is my worst enemy. What am I?' A snowman. Children like riddles they can picture.

What is a hard Christmas riddle?

Try: 'Which Christmas word turns into a man's name when you read it backwards?' Noel, which reversed is Leon. Or: 'The more of me you take off on Christmas morning, the bigger the pile on the floor. What am I?' Wrapping paper.

How do you play a Christmas riddle game?

Split into teams, read one riddle aloud, and give 30 seconds to answer. If nobody gets it, give one hint and 30 more seconds. Score 2 points for an answer without the hint and 1 with it. Play ten riddles, easy to hard.

How do you write your own Christmas riddle?

Pick an answer, such as a candle. List what it does and looks like without naming it: it gets shorter the longer it works, it cries wax. Write two or three of those as 'I' sentences and end with 'What am I?' Test it on one person first.
