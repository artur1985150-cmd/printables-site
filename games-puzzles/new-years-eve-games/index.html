In short

  • Plan games by the hour: easy ones while guests arrive, short challenges after 10 p.m., and one game that ends at midnight.
  • One-minute challenges keep energy up in the slow hour before the countdown; play one every half hour.
  • Every game below lists how many can play, what you need, a time limit and how to win.
  • Pen-and-paper games about the year (resolutions, predictions, a quiz about the group) suit adults and teens.
  • If children join the Twelve Grapes at midnight, cut the grapes: the CDC lists uncut grapes as a choking hazard for young children.

The best New Year's Eve games fit the hour you play them. Start with easy pen-and-paper games while guests arrive, switch to one-minute challenges after 10 p.m., when energy dips, and finish with a game that ends exactly at midnight, such as musical chairs or a balloon countdown. Below are 20 games we wrote, each with players, supplies, a time limit and a winner.

How to plan New Year's Eve games through the night

A New Year's Eve party is longer than most, and the games should follow its shape. Tribute's guide plays a minute-to-win-it round about every half hour after 10 p.m. and sets its board game final for 11:40. We use the same idea and split the evening into four slots:

Slot What works Length
Arrival, 8 to 9:30 p.m. Games people can join alone or in pairs Running in the background
Main game, 9:30 to 10:30 p.m. One team game for the whole room 20 to 30 minutes
Late evening, 10:30 to 11:45 p.m. One-minute challenges, one every half hour 10 minutes each
Countdown, 11:45 to midnight A game that ends on the stroke of twelve 10 to 15 minutes

Families with small children can squeeze the same plan into 6 to 9 p.m. and finish with an early countdown.

Games for when guests arrive

These run in the background while people come in, take coats and find a drink.

  1. New Year bingo. Make cards with squares for things that happen during the evening: someone mentions a resolution, a toast is made, someone says "where did the year go", a song from this year plays, a cork pops. Players mark squares all night. First full row picks the countdown song. Any number of players.
  2. Prediction board. A poster titled with next year and a pack of sticky notes. Guests write one prediction about the group or the world. Photograph it before midnight and read it out next New Year's Eve.
  3. Whose resolution? Each guest writes a resolution on a slip, folds it and drops it in a bowl. Keep the bowl for the main game slot.
  4. Year in a drawing. Each guest draws their year in a single picture on an index card, no words. Pin them up; later, the room guesses who drew which.

Pen-and-paper games about the year

These work best in the main slot, with everyone seated and drinks topped up.

  1. Guess whose resolution. Read the slips from the bowl one by one. Everyone writes down who they think wrote it. One point per correct guess; the writer gets a point for each person they fooled. Ten to twenty minutes, four or more players.
  2. The group's year quiz. Before the party, write 15 questions about things your group did this year: who went where, who started what, which film everyone argued about. Teams of three to five write answers. One point each; the host's answer is final. Our Christmas trivia questions explain how to run a team quiz smoothly.
  3. Three words for the year. Everyone writes three words that sum up their year. Read them out in random order; the room guesses whose they are.
  4. Headline charades. Write past-year headlines from your own lives on slips ("Sam finally learns to drive"). Act them out with the usual charades rules. Our Christmas charades page has the full rules and timing.
  5. Would you rather, New Year edition. Two-way choices about next year: travel more or save more, learn a language or an instrument, a quiet New Year's Day or a big one. Everyone picks and one person says why. Our Christmas would you rather page explains four ways to play.

One-minute challenges for the hour before midnight

Play Party Plan collects 16 of these and Tribute adds a few, but most come without a time limit or a winner. Ours all run for 60 seconds, one player at a time unless noted. The fastest or best score wins a small prize.

  1. Countdown cup stack. Twelve plastic cups. Build a pyramid, knock it down and restack the cups in a single tower before the minute is up.
  2. Balloon keep-up. Keep two balloons in the air for 60 seconds without holding them. For the final, add a third balloon.
  3. Blower race. Push a ping pong ball from one end of a table to the other using only a party blower. Two players race side by side.
  4. Hat knock-off. Line up six paper party hats at the end of a table. Bounce ping pong balls to knock them off; most hats down in a minute wins.
  5. Clock sort. Write the numbers 1 to 12 on separate slips, mix them in a bowl, and lay them out in a clock face on the table before time runs out.
  6. Confetti scoop. Using only a spoon held in your mouth, move as much confetti as you can from one bowl to another in a minute. Weigh or compare the piles.

Games that end at midnight

The last game should finish on the countdown, not run through it.

  1. Midnight musical chairs. Start at about 11:50 with one chair fewer than players. Remove a chair each round. Time it so the last two players are left when the countdown begins; they share the first toast.
  2. Twelve grapes race. Each player gets a cup of twelve grapes. On the twelve strokes of midnight, eat one grape per stroke. Finish before the last stroke and you win the year's luck. According to Wikipedia, this is a Spanish tradition dating to at least 1895, with each grape standing for a month of the new year. For children, the CDC lists uncut grapes among choking hazards for young children, so cut them small or use another fruit.
  3. Balloon countdown. Write a task on a slip for each hour from 6 p.m. to 11 p.m. ("start a dance-off", "make a toast", "tell your best moment of the year"), put each in a balloon and pop one every hour. The last balloon, popped at midnight, holds confetti.
  4. Guess the time. Hide every clock and phone at 11:30. Everyone writes the time they think it is when the host calls out. Closest guess pours the midnight drinks.

New Year's Eve games for kids

Children need short games and an early peak. These work for an early midnight at 8 or 9 p.m.:

  • Noisemaker hunt (20). Hide the horns and blowers around one room. Children may use whatever they find at the countdown.
  • Balloon keep-up and countdown cup stack from the challenges above.
  • Freeze dance to the songs of the year: stop the music and anyone still moving sits out.
  • Party hat build-off, as Tribute suggests: paper, tape, stickers and tinsel, twenty minutes, and a grandparent judges.
  • An early countdown: replay a midnight countdown on screen, with poppers and a toast in plastic cups.

Prizes, scoring and keeping it fun

Keep score on one sheet for the whole night, so the bingo winner, the quiz team and the challenge champions all add up to one overall winner by 11:45. Small prizes are enough: a party hat, a chocolate coin, first pick of the midnight snacks.

A few rules that save arguments:

  • The host's answer is final in any quiz.
  • Anyone can sit a round out, especially the physical challenges.
  • Drinking games, if you play them, stay short: one round near the end beats a whole evening of them, and keep good soft drinks for anyone who prefers them.
  • Clear breakables off the table before balloon or ping pong games.

Questions people ask

What games can you play on New Year's Eve?

Pen-and-paper games such as guessing whose resolution is whose, one-minute challenges with balloons, cups or ping pong balls, a quiz about the group's year, and a countdown game that ends at midnight, such as musical chairs or a ball race. Families can add a noisemaker hunt and an early countdown at 9 p.m.

What are good New Year's Eve games for a big group?

Bingo with squares for things that happen during the evening, a quiz played in teams of four to six, and guess-whose-resolution read out to the whole room. Each one works for twenty people as easily as for eight, because everyone plays at once.

What New Year's Eve games can kids play?

A noisemaker hunt, balloon keep-up, party hat making, freeze dance and a countdown cup stack. Play them before an early midnight at 9 p.m. so children get the confetti and the countdown before bed.

What is the twelve grapes tradition?

A Spanish custom of eating one grape with each of the twelve strokes of the clock at midnight on 31 December, one for each month of the coming year. Wikipedia dates it to at least 1895. As a party game, the first person to finish all twelve before the last stroke wins.

How many games do you need for a New Year's Eve party?

Four or five, plus a countdown game. A party from 8 p.m. to midnight has about four hours; a game of 15 to 20 minutes each hour, with talk and food in between, fills it without turning the night into a tournament.
