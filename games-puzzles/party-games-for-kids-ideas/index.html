The best party games for kids have few rules and start fast: musical statues, Simon says, What's the time Mr Wolf and pass the parcel for little ones; scavenger hunts, charades and party bingo for children who can read. Choose by age and space, keep a quiet table for breaks, and skip balloon games if guests are under 8.

Further down are printable games, and the balloon and food points most party lists leave out.

How to pick games for your party

Childhood101's advice for party games is to choose simple ones with few rules that are quick to play, so nobody waits around, and ones that need little preparation and equipment you already have. Games don't have to match the party theme.

Before you pick, answer these:

  1. How old are the guests? Games with reading or scoring suit school-age children; the youngest do best with copying and moving to music.
  2. How much space? A living room, a garden, a hall.
  3. How much can you prepare? Some games need only music; printable ones need an evening with a printer and scissors.

GoodTo lists musical statues for ages 2 and up, and suggests a toddler version of pass the parcel with a small treat between the layers. For this age, let every child win something: a sticker in each layer works better than one prize in the middle.

Party games for kids 6 to 9

The age bands here and below are our suggestions; mixed groups often play across them.

  • Scavenger hunt with picture or word clues.
  • Freeze tag or stuck in the mud outdoors.
  • Musical chairs, if you have space for a circle of chairs.
  • Sack race with pillowcases, outdoors on grass.
  • Pin the tail on the donkey, or a version drawn to fit your theme.
  • Minute to win it: short one-minute challenges with cups, spoons or paper.

Party games for older kids (8 to 12)

  • Charades with printed cards.
  • Team scavenger hunt with written riddles.
  • Party bingo with prompts instead of numbers ("find someone born in summer").
  • Murder mystery or escape-room style puzzles at the table.
  • Tug of war on soft ground.

Indoor games with no equipment

For a small flat or a rainy day:

  • Simon says.
  • Musical statues or musical bumps.
  • Sleeping lions: everyone lies still; the one who moves helps catch the others.
  • I spy.
  • Telephone: whisper a sentence round a circle and compare the ending with the start.
  • Heads down, thumbs up for a class-sized group.

Printable party games

Printable games suit children who can read, or mixed groups where an adult reads aloud. Games that work well printed:

  • Scavenger hunt clue cards, each leading to the next hiding place.
  • Party bingo with pictures for non-readers and words for readers.
  • Charades or "act it out" cards.
  • I spy sheets for a quiet table.
  • Word searches and mazes on the party theme.
  • Would you rather cards.
  • Pin the tail (or the horn, or the hat) on a printed poster.

To set them up, print cards on card stock so they survive many hands, cut them on a trimmer for straight edges, and keep each game in its own envelope. A colouring or dot marker activities table gives children who want a break something calm to do; for preschool guests, a few pages from a busy book work the same way. Balloons: when to leave them out

Many lists feature balloon stomp, pass the balloon and water balloon toss. Before you plan one, know this: the American Academy of Pediatrics says balloons pose a choking risk to children up to 8 years of age, and lists balloons among the small round objects that cause the most choking deaths. The CPSC warns that uninflated balloons and fragments from popped balloons can choke and kill a small child.

If you do use balloons:

  • Adults blow them up, never children.
  • Pick up every popped piece straight away.
  • Keep uninflated balloons in a drawer, not on the party table.
  • For guests under 8, play balloon games with soft balls or bubbles instead.

Food games and prizes

Bobbing for apples, doughnut-on-a-string and sweet-eating races are on many lists. The AAP lists foods to keep from young children until age 4 or older, including hard, gooey or sticky candy, whole grapes, popcorn, marshmallows, raw fruit chunks such as apple pieces, nuts and chewing gum. It also says children should never run, walk, play or lie down with food in their mouths. That rules out eating mixed with running games for little guests.

For prizes, think about younger siblings too. The CPSC says toys made for children 3 and older should be kept away from young children, and the NHS advises keeping small toy parts out of babies' reach. Stickers, bubbles or a printed certificate are easy prizes for a mixed-age group.

Questions people ask

What are good party games for kids?

Games with few rules that start quickly: musical statues, Simon says, What's the time Mr Wolf, pass the parcel, a scavenger hunt, charades and party bingo. Pick by the children's ages and how much space you have.

How many games do you need for a kids' party?

There's no fixed number. Plan more short games than you think you need, put the most active ones early, and keep a calm table or a story for when energy drops.

What party games need no equipment?

Simon says, musical statues (you only need music), What's the time Mr Wolf, sleeping lions, I spy and freeze tag all work with nothing but players.

Are balloon games safe for young kids?

The American Academy of Pediatrics says balloons pose a choking risk to children up to 8 years of age, and the CPSC warns that uninflated balloons and pieces of popped ones can choke a small child. For younger guests, swap balloon games for soft balls or bubbles.

What are good printable party games for kids?

Scavenger hunt clue cards, party bingo, charades or 'act it out' cards, I spy sheets, word searches, mazes and 'would you rather' cards. Print on card stock so they survive many hands.
