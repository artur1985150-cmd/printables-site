How the survey game works

Family Feud is an American TV game show created by Mark Goodson, first aired on ABC on July 12, 1976, according to Wikipedia. This page is not connected to the show; it borrows the general format, which goes like this on TV:

  • Two families of five play against each other.
  • Each question was put to a survey of 100 people, and the hidden answers on the board are ranked by how many people gave them.
  • A round opens with a face-off: one player from each side, first to buzz in answers. The higher-ranked answer wins control.
  • The side in control answers in turn. A wrong answer is a strike; after three strikes, the other side can steal the round's points with one correct answer.
  • In the bonus round, two players from the winning side answer the same five quick questions one after the other, against the clock, and try to reach 200 points between them.

House rules for playing at home

You don't need a buzzer or a board. You need a host who doesn't play, a sheet with the answers that the teams can't see, and a phone timer.

  1. Make two teams of two to five. Seat them facing each other.
  2. Face-off: one player from each team. The host reads the question; the first to slap the table answers. If it's the top answer, their team takes control. If not, the other player answers, and the higher-ranked answer wins.
  3. The team in control goes round its players, one answer each. Happy Mom Hacks gives each player five seconds; it keeps things moving.
  4. Three strikes and the other team confers and gives one steal answer. If it's on the board, they take the round's points.
  5. Rotate face-off players every round, so everyone gets a turn.
  6. Close enough counts: "lights" and "fairy lights" are the same answer. The host decides, and the host's word is final. Garland and Gather gives the host the same final say.

Play five rounds, then a fast round to finish (rules below).

Christmas Family Feud questions with answers

Twenty questions for a mixed family group. Answers are listed from what we expect to be most popular to least.

  1. Name something people put on a Christmas tree. Lights, ornaments, a star, tinsel, an angel.
  2. Name something Santa needs on Christmas Eve. Reindeer, his sleigh, a map, milk and cookies, his sack.
  3. Name a Christmas film everyone has seen. Home Alone, Elf, The Grinch, A Christmas Carol, The Polar Express.
  4. Name something you'd find in a Christmas stocking. Sweets, an orange or satsuma, small toys, socks, a chocolate coin.
  5. Name a food served at Christmas dinner. Turkey, ham, roast potatoes, stuffing, Brussels sprouts.
  6. Name one of Santa's reindeer. Rudolph, Dasher, Comet, Blitzen, Prancer.
  7. Name something that goes on top of a Christmas tree. A star, an angel, a bow, a Santa hat, a snowflake.
  8. Name a Christmas song people sing along to. Jingle Bells, All I Want for Christmas Is You, Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer, Silent Night, Last Christmas.
  9. Name something people do on Christmas Eve. Wrap presents, leave out cookies, watch a film, go to church, open one present.
  10. Name something you need to wrap a present. Paper, tape, scissors, a bow, a tag.
  11. Name something that's red at Christmas. Santa's suit, Rudolph's nose, holly berries, a stocking, a bow.
  12. Name a reason to go to the shops on Christmas Eve. A forgotten present, batteries, food, wrapping paper, tape.
  13. Name something you build or make out of snow. A snowman, snowballs, a snow angel, a fort, an igloo.
  14. Name a Christmas dessert. Christmas pudding, gingerbread, Yule log, pie, mince pies.
  15. Name something people hang on their front door. A wreath, lights, a bow, mistletoe, a sign.
  16. Name something that always goes wrong at Christmas dinner. Something burns, the turkey isn't ready, someone argues, someone spills a drink, too much food.
  17. Name something you pretend to like when you unwrap it. Socks, a jumper, a book, soap, a mug.
  18. Name a Christmas character who isn't Santa. Rudolph, the Grinch, Frosty, Mrs Claus, an elf.
  19. Name something people do on Boxing Day or the day after Christmas. Eat leftovers, sleep, shop the sales, go for a walk, watch TV.
  20. Name a New Year's resolution people make over Christmas dinner. Exercise more, eat less, save money, drink less, read more.

Questions for kids

Six with answers children are likely to know. Let small children answer as a team.

  1. Name an animal you think of at Christmas. Reindeer, a penguin, a polar bear, a robin, a donkey.
  2. Name something Santa's elves do. Make toys, wrap presents, feed the reindeer, check the list, help Santa.
  3. Name something you leave out for Santa. Cookies, milk, carrots, a note, mince pies.
  4. Name something cold. Snow, ice, ice cream, the North Pole, a snowman.
  5. Name a colour you see at Christmas. Red, green, gold, white, silver.
  6. Name a present every kid wants. Toys, a bike, a game, sweets, a phone.

Questions for adults and the office

These work at a work party: nothing too personal, no drinking. For seated games that suit colleagues, see our Christmas party games list.

  1. Name something people do at the office Christmas party. Eat, dance, give speeches, take photos, leave early.
  2. Name an excuse for eating another mince pie. It's Christmas, they'll go to waste, I skipped lunch, just one more, someone made them.
  3. Name something people fall asleep in front of after Christmas dinner. The TV, a film, the fire, the King's or Queen's speech, a board game.
  4. Name a job that's hardest in December. Postal worker, shop assistant, delivery driver, Santa, chef.
  5. Name something that makes a Secret Santa gift go wrong. Too cheap, a joke gift, the wrong size, a regift, the wrong name.
  6. Name a word you'd use for your out-of-office reply over Christmas. Holiday, away, back in January, festive, offline.

A fast round to finish

On TV, two players from the winning side answer the bonus questions in a set time, the first player alone, the second without hearing the first's answers. At home, send the second player out of the room. Give the first 20 seconds and the second 25, the limits Wikipedia gives for the show since 1994, and repeat answers don't count for the second player.

  1. Name a Christmas drink. Hot chocolate, eggnog, mulled wine, cider, coffee.
  2. Name something you hang on a Christmas tree that isn't a bauble. Lights, tinsel, candy canes, a star, popcorn strings.
  3. Name a word that rhymes with "snow". Go, know, slow, glow, toe.
  4. Name something you do on Christmas morning. Open presents, eat breakfast, call family, go to church, play with toys.
  5. Name something you lose every Christmas. The tape end, the remote, batteries, the scissors, your temper.

If they reach a set score together, say 100 with the fixed scale below, they win a small prize.

How to get real points: survey your guests

This is the step most game pages skip. On TV, an answer's points equal the number of people out of 100 who gave it. You can do the same with your own crowd.

  1. A few days before, send the questions you plan to use to everyone coming, by message or a free online form, and ask for the first answer that comes to mind.
  2. Ask people not to discuss them.
  3. Count how many people gave each answer. Merge close matches: "tree lights" and "lights" are one answer.
  4. Keep the top five per question. Each answer scores one point per person who gave it.
  5. Write the counts on the host's sheet.

Guests also hear their own answers revealed, which is half the fun. With 15 guests, the top answer might score 9 and the fifth only 1; that spread is real, and it makes the steal round tense.

No time for a survey? Use a fixed scale on our lists: top answer 30 points, then 25, 20, 15 and 10. It isn't survey data, so tell the players.

Questions people ask

How do you play Christmas Family Feud at home?

Split into two teams and pick a host. One player from each team faces off on a question; whoever names the higher-ranked answer wins control. That team keeps guessing until it finds every answer or gets three wrong, then the other team can steal the points with one guess. Highest total after five rounds wins.

Where do the answers in Christmas Family Feud come from?

On the TV show, answers come from a survey of 100 people. Printable and online lists, including ours, are usually the writer's own guesses. For real answers, survey your guests: send the questions a few days before and count how many people gave each answer.

How many questions do you need for Christmas Family Feud?

Five rounds is a good length, so every player can take a face-off, plus a few spares in case a question falls flat and four or five for a bonus round. This page has 37, enough for a family round, a kids round and an office round.

What are funny Christmas Family Feud questions?

Questions where the answers make people laugh at themselves: 'Something you pretend to like when you unwrap it', 'An excuse for eating another mince pie' or 'Something that always goes wrong at Christmas dinner'.

Is this the official Family Feud game?

No. Family Feud is a TV game show, and this page has no connection to it; it describes a survey-style party game played at home.
