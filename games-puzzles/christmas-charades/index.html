In short

  • Act a Christmas word, film or song in silence while your team guesses before the timer runs out.
  • The prompts below come in three levels: easy objects and actions for children, medium titles, and hard phrases for adults.
  • Agree the hand signals first: fingers for the number of words, a tug of the ear for 'sounds like', a cranked camera for a film.
  • One point per correct guess; Wikipedia also describes a version where the other team scores when you fail.
  • Let an actor pass once per turn, so a hard card does not stall the game.

Christmas charades is the old miming game with holiday prompts: one player acts out a Christmas word, film or song without speaking, and their team guesses before time runs out. Below are 90 prompts sorted into easy, medium and hard, the hand signals players use, and short rules you can read out before the first turn.

How to play Christmas charades

You need slips of paper, a bowl or a Santa hat to draw from, a timer and someone to keep score.

  1. Split the group into two teams. With a very small group, everyone plays on one side against the clock, as WhiteElephantRules.com suggests.
  2. Write the prompts on slips, fold them and put them in the bowl.
  3. A player from team one draws a slip, reads it silently and starts acting when the timer starts.
  4. The team shouts guesses. The actor may not speak, mouth words, spell letters in the air or point at objects in the room.
  5. A correct guess before time runs out scores one point. Then team two takes a turn.
  6. Play until everyone has acted the same number of times. The team with more points wins.

Timers differ from page to page: WhiteElephantRules.com gives the actor one minute, Make Life Lovely sets three. One minute suits adults with the medium and hard prompts; give children and first-timers two or three.

Charades hand signals to agree first

Wikipedia lists a set of signals that most players know. Go through them before the first turn, because a quick signal saves the actor half the minute.

What you want to say Signal
Number of words Hold up that many fingers
Number of syllables Lay that many fingers on the inside of the opposite elbow
Film Crank an old movie camera beside your head
Song or carol Hold one arm out and pretend to sing
Book Open both palms like a book
TV show Draw a rectangle in the air with both index fingers
Sounds like Tug or point at your earlobe
A small word (a, of, in) Hold finger and thumb close together
"The" Make a T with your hands

Scoring and ties

The simplest score is one point for each correct guess. One Simple Party adds a steal: if the acting team misses, the other team gets one guess for a point. Wikipedia describes a version where a team also earns a point for every answer the other side fails to guess in time.

For a tie, WhiteElephantRules.com plays sudden-death rounds: both teams act one card each, and the round ends when one team scores and the other doesn't.

When the actor is stuck, and close guesses

None of the game pages we read says what to do with an impossible card, so here is what we suggest:

  • One pass per turn. The actor puts the slip back, draws another, and the timer keeps running.
  • A guess counts when it has every main word in the right order. "Silent Night" counts for "Silent Night"; "Quiet Night" does not.
  • Small words such as "the" and "a" don't have to be said, which matches the Wikipedia note that groups often leave short words out unless they are part of a longer phrase.
  • If the actor speaks, that card scores nothing. Keep it light: a laugh, a groan, next card.

Christmas charades on a video call

Charades works over video because it is all gesture. Send each actor their prompts privately in a chat message before the call, set the timer on your own screen, and ask the actor to step back so their whole body is in frame. One Simple Party suggests building the guest list in advance for a virtual game; split it into two teams before the call so nobody waits while you sort names.

Questions people ask

How do you play Christmas charades?

Split into two teams and put prompt slips in a bowl. One player draws a slip and acts it out without speaking while their team guesses before the timer ends, usually one to three minutes. A correct guess scores a point, then the other team takes a turn.

What are good Christmas charades ideas for kids?

Things a child can show with their body: building a snowman, wrapping a present, a reindeer, ice skating, hanging a stocking, eating a cookie, a snowball fight. Pair children who can't read yet with an older player who whispers the prompt.

What are the hand signals in charades?

Fingers held up for the number of words, fingers on the forearm for syllables, a tug of the earlobe for 'sounds like', both hands opened like a book, a cranked old camera for a film, an arm out as if singing for a song, and a finger on the nose for 'correct'.

What are funny Christmas charades for adults?

Scenes that need a bit of acting: untangling the lights, pretending to love an ugly sweater, assembling a toy at 2 a.m., the last-minute Christmas Eve dash to the shop, or a cat knocking over the tree. They get laughs because everyone has lived them.
